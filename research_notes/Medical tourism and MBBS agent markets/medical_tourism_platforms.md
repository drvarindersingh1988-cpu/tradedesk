# Medical Tourism Facilitator Platforms Bringing Central Asian / Russian-Speaking (CIS) Patients to India — Competitor Map (as of Oct 2026)

> Method note for the report writer: research ran on 14 Oct 2026. WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every site tried (otzovik, bookimed, vaidam, kursiv, maxhealthcare, citybiz, kyivpost, medturist.kz). Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result summaries and snippets, not from full-page reads. Before the shared web-search budget ran out (about 37 searches), several named companies returned no usable results. Treat single-source figures as indicative. Anything dated before 2024 is flagged **[pre-2024, possibly outdated]**.

## 1. Global / multi-country platforms and Russian-language aggregators active with CIS patients (Bookimed, MediGlobus, PlacidWay, Qunomedical, Medigo, WhatClinic, RU-language agencies)

### Takeaway
Bookimed (Ukrainian-founded in 2014) is the only global platform with clear scale and a deliberate Russian- and Kazakh-language operation. Booking value was reported at about $22M GMV in 2025 across about 1,700 clinics, with a free-to-patient, clinic-commission model, and Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan are named among its main client countries. Below it, the Russian-language India market is a long tail of small agencies (Kazakh, Russian and Uzbek) and SEO microsites. They are mostly unrated and unaudited, they offer the same package of services, and they show almost no verifiable scale.

### Cited Findings

**Market context: how big the Central Asian flow to India is**
- India's 2025 medical-purpose foreign arrivals by source: Bangladesh 325,127; Iraq 30,989; **Uzbekistan 13,699 (3rd)**; Somalia 11,506; **Turkmenistan 10,231 (5th)**; Oman 9,738; Kenya 9,357. Total 2025: 507,244 — [PIB/Govt of India document, May 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/may/doc202652862301.pdf); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/medical-tourism-over-18-lakh-foreigners-visited-india-from-176-countries-in-two-years-enn26080406031)
- Medical visas issued by India fell 22%, from 5.97 lakh (2023) to 4.64 lakh (2024), mainly because of Bangladesh unrest. Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan were among the countries whose medical visas *increased* in 2024 — [Factly](https://factly.in/data-medical-visas-issued-by-india-down-more-than-20-in-2024-on-account-of-political-unrest-in-bangladesh/)
- Uzbekistan is reported at 8,921 medical travellers in 2024, rising to 13,699 in 2025. The 2024 figure is unverified, and one promotional site attributes 8,921 to 2025 instead — [Medical Tourism Watch](https://medicaltourismwatch.com/india-top-medical-tourism-source-countries/); [Shifam Health](https://shifamhealth.com/medical-visa-uzbekistan-to-india/)
- About 8,900 Uzbek arrivals are reported for early 2025 (Jan–Apr parliamentary data context) — [Tashkent Medical Academy repository](https://repo.tma.uz/xmlui/handle/1/3572); [Ministry of Tourism Lok Sabha answer](https://tourism.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-08/usq.2219%20for%2007.08.pdf)
- No country-wise India medical-arrival figures were found for Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan or Russia. Those countries do not appear in the top 7 for 2025 — [PIB 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/may/doc202652862301.pdf)
- Kazakhstanis' top outbound medical destinations are Turkey (>15,000/yr), South Korea (~12,500/yr) and Israel, per the Astana Development Center. India is not in the top 3. The quote surfaced via [Khabar](https://khabar.kz/ru/news/zdravookhranenie/169062-meditsinskij-turizm-v-kazakhstane-nabiraet-oboroty) / [24.kz](https://24.kz/ru/news/social/640020-meditsinskij-turizm-chto-lechat-inostrantsy-v-kazakhstane); I could not open the pages to confirm which article carries it.
- Kazakhstan's state-funded treatment abroad is small. In 2024, 80 people (67 children) were treated in Turkey, Germany, Russia, Belarus and **India** — [cmn.kz](https://cmn.kz/lechenie-grazhdan-za-rubezhom/). Applications go through local health authorities, not intermediaries — [eGov.kz](https://egov.kz/cms/ru/services/health_care/pass-132-5_mz)
- Russians' top medical destinations in 2026 are Turkey, Belarus and Thailand, with South Korea growing fastest. India is mentioned mainly for traditional-medicine and recovery programmes, not as a main surgical destination — [Inc. Russia, 2026](https://incrussia.ru/news/meditsinskij-turizm-v-2026-m-novoe-napravlenie-kuda-rossiyane-edut-ne-otdyhat/)

Crunchbase also lists Nika Tech Family — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/bookimed-com); [StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/bookimed); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/222869-53) **[pre-2024 round]**

- Business model:
  - Free to patients, with no surcharge; the patient pays the clinic's own bill.

  - Deposits are deducted from the treatment price and are refundable if requested at least 10 days before the visit.
  - Prices are provisional: materials, tests and extra nights are often billed separately.
  - Sources: [Finance policy](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/page/finance-policy/); [Trust & policies](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/page/policies/); [Partnership page (DE)](https://de.bookimed.com/doc/partnership); [What you can expect](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/page/what-you-can-expect-from-bookimed/)

### Inferences
- Bookimed is the benchmark CIS competitor.
  - Its moats are SEO depth in Russian and Kazakh, a coordinator bench, about 1,000 Trustpilot reviews, and a multi-country catalogue that lets it steer a Kazakh patient to Turkey or Korea instead of India.
  - Its weak points, from the complaints, are steering toward partner clinics, prices above going direct, and coordinators who are non-physician salespeople.
  - A dual-licensed Russian-speaking physician directly counters the "salesperson coordinator" weakness.
  - A Ukrainian-founded brand may be less trusted by some Russian-federation patients. This is speculation; I found no evidence of it.
- Bookimed's ~$22M GMV spans all countries. If India's share is in the order of 10–20%, which is a guess, its India GMV would be only a few million dollars. That suggests the CIS→India niche is not dominated by any well-funded player.
- The demand signal is strongest from **Uzbekistan (13.7k) and Turkmenistan (10.2k)**. Kazakhstan and Russia send fewer patients to India and prefer Turkey, Korea and Israel. A Kazakhstan-licensed founder will need to sell India *against* Turkey and Korea in Kazakhstan, while Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan are already India-leaning.
- The Russian-language long tail (medturist.kz, Easymedcare, ФортисМЕД, Avanta and others) shows no reviews, no named doctors and no scale. That is a low trust floor for a credentialed entrant to beat.

### Gaps
- Bookimed's exact commission rate, its India share of GMV, the number of Indian partner hospitals, and CIS patient volumes: none found.
- Bookimed's 2025 GMV ($22.1M) has only a single press-release-style source.

## 2. India-based facilitators (Vaidam, Lyfboat, Medmonks, IndianMedTrip, MediFly, Medsurge, Forerunners, Credihealth, Healthtrip, Mediyatra, etc.) and hospital-owned CIS channels

### Takeaway
India-side facilitators are small, lightly funded companies whose revenue comes from hospital commissions (Vaidam ~$74K seed, Credihealth ~$1.5M, Healthtrip ~$5M Series A). Vaidam is the clearest one targeting Uzbekistan, through Russian and Uzbek landing pages and a stated Uzbek source market. The bigger competitive threat in Central Asia is the hospitals going direct: Max and Medanta have Tashkent offices, Aakash owns a Tashkent hospital, Yashoda signed a referral MoU with Turkistan city (KZ) in Sept 2026, and Lilavati is opening an OPD in Kazakhstan.

## 3. How these platforms win patients (SEO in Russian, Instagram/Telegram/WhatsApp, ads, local-doctor referrals, diaspora agents)

### Takeaway
The visible acquisition engines are Russian-language SEO, both programmatic and content-led, and self-published "Top 10" rankings and press releases. Underneath sits a less visible layer of Russian-speaking brokers and interpreters tied to Delhi hospitals. In-country, the channels are business directories (2GIS, Top.uz, GoldenPages), hospital rep offices, embassy and B2B events, and government MoUs. I found no direct evidence about Telegram or Instagram campaigns or kickbacks to referring doctors.

## 4. Which features are common vs rare (price transparency, fixed packages, doctor-led review, post-discharge teleconsult follow-up, escrow, outcome data, independent reviews)

### Takeaway
Almost everyone offers the same features: free to the patient, paid by hospital commission, free quotes, visa invitation, airport pickup, interpreter and accommodation. Rare or absent features are binding fixed prices, escrow or payment protection, published outcome data, structured post-return teleconsult follow-up, and genuine physician-led case review. Bookimed itself states that its prices are provisional and final bills can be higher.

### Cited Findings
- **Free to patient, hospital pays commission:** Bookimed ([finance policy](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/page/finance-policy/)); Lyfboat ([hospital FAQs](https://www.lyfboat.com/hospital-faqs/))
- **Reported commission levels (vendor sources, weak evidence):**
  - 7.5%–30% depending on facilitator volume and procedure, with mid-size facilitators at about 15–20% — [My1Health referral-partner guide](https://my1health.com/referral-partner-network/articles/steps-to-partnering-with-international-hospitals-for-medical-tourism)
  - Indian hospitals pay roughly 20–35% of treatment value — [iChelon Consulting, 2026](https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/medical-tourism-marketing-india-hospitals-2026)
  - Fees are typically paid after treatment is complete — [My1Health](https://my1health.com/referral-partner-network/articles/steps-to-partnering-with-international-hospitals-for-medical-tourism)
  - The practice is "criticised and even outlawed in some jurisdictions" but remains the industry's main model — [Wikipedia: Medical tourism agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism_agent)
  - These attributions come from search snippets and could not be verified on the pages.
- **Hidden markup risk:** a pricing consultant alleges hospitals inflate quotes given to facilitators to cover commission. Her example: a $25,000 procedure quoted at $30,000–35,000 — [Maria Todd, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/real-truth-medical-tourism-prices-abroad-maria-k-todd-mha-phd). This is one expert's claim, not measured data.
- **Hospital in-house international desks** reportedly charge $500–1,500 for coordination but refer only to their own facility — [MedicalTourismPackages](https://www.medicaltourismpackages.com/what-is-a-medical-tourism-facilitator/) (search-snippet attribution)
- **Price transparency:**
  - Bookimed shows prices, but they are explicitly provisional. Materials, extra procedures, pre-op tests and extra nights are billed separately. It promises price parity with direct patients and says some clinics give discounts — [Bookimed finance policy](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/page/finance-policy/); [What you can expect](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/page/what-you-can-expect-from-bookimed/)

- **Doctor involvement:**
  - Bookimed reportedly has 65 medical specialists alongside 115 staff — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/bookimed)
  - MediGlobus reviews cite an in-house doctor available 24/7 — [Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/mediglobus.com)

  - None was found that advertises a licensed physician in the patient's home country.
- **Post-discharge follow-up:** Max's Tashkent centre lists "follow-up care", but only for Max hospitals — [Max Healthcare](https://www.maxhealthcare.in/international/en/center/max-healthcare-patient-assistance-center-uzbekistan). I found no facilitator advertising structured teleconsult follow-up after return.
- **Accreditation of the facilitator:** Lyfboat has NABH medical-travel-facilitator accreditation — [ITIJ](https://www.itij.com/latest/news/lyfboat-achieves-nabh-accreditation). It is rare among competitors found.

  - MediGlobus: ~92 ([Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/mediglobus.com))

  - Medsurge: 2 ([Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/medsurgeindia.com))
  - Avanta Tashkent: 0 ([Top.uz](https://top.uz/company/avanta-lechenie-v-indii))

## 5. Known failures, controversies, lawsuits

### Takeaway
I found no lawsuits or regulatory actions against Bookimed, Vaidam, Lyfboat or other named CIS-facing facilitators. The documented risks are patient complaints (steering, price inflation, poor interpreters, a junior doctor instead of the professor paid for), Kazakhstan MoH warnings (Aug 2026) about fraudulent foreign-clinic "representatives", and India's recurring kidney-transplant rackets run through "medical tourism" fronts. None of the rackets found involved Central Asian patients.

### Cited Findings
- **Kazakhstan MoH, Aug 2026:** warns patients to be wary of guaranteed-cure promises, unproven methods, and pressure to decide fast without document review. Advises checking JCI/ISO accreditation, doctor qualifications and price transparency. State-funded treatment abroad goes through health authorities, not intermediaries — [Kursiv, 11 Aug 2026](https://kz.kursiv.media/2026-08-11/lsbs-kak-vybrat-kliniku-za-rubezhom-i-ne-popast-k-moshennikam-sovety-minzdrava/); [Otyrar.kz, Aug 2026](https://otyrar.kz/2026/08/kazahstanczev-prizvali-proveryat-zarubezhnye-kliniki-pered-lecheniem/); [Qumash.kz](https://qumash.kz/news/kazahstantsam-rasskazali-kak-proverit-zarubezhnie-kliniki-pered-lecheniem-1a1991/)
- **Kazakh guidance on spotting fake clinic "representatives"** — [MedMost](https://medmost.org/kak-proverit-chto-predstavitel-zarubezhnoj-kliniki-ne-moshennik/)
- **Kazakhstan domestic probes:** clinics investigated for not providing services after receiving state budget money. This concerns domestic clinics, not facilitators — [Azattyq Asia](https://www.azattyqasia.org/a/v-kazahstane-rassleduyut-dela-v-otnoshenii-klinik-za-neokazanie-meduslug-posle-polucheniya-deneg-iz-gosbyudzheta/33820687.html)
- **Typical Russian-language intermediary fraud patterns** (documented for Israel, but the mechanics apply generally):
  - A low initial quote that later rises.
  - Intermediaries posing as the hospital or as a charity.
  - Promises of a cure in terminal cases, with large prepayments.
  - A narrow doctor network.
  - Sources: [Isramedic](https://isramedic.co.il/index.php/lechenie-v-izraile-obmanyvayut); [Asclepius-kids](https://www.asclepius-kids.com/post/lecheniebezposrednikov); [assuta-clinic.org](https://www.assuta-clinic.org/kak-obmanyvayut-pri-lechenii-v-izraile/)
- **Bookimed complaints:** steering toward partner clinics, refusal to work with the patient's chosen clinic, and a price about 1.5× higher than elsewhere — [Otzovik](https://otzovik.com/reviews/ru_bookimed_com-mezhdunarodniy_centr_podderzhki_pacientov/). A Kazakh patient criticised the Russian of Indian staff (on-platform review; search snippet).
- **Apollo via an intermediary:** a reviewer described "coordination and translation of dubious quality" and said he got a regular doctor instead of the professor he paid for — [hospitals-travel.ru](https://hospitals-travel.ru/countries/india)
- **Kidney rackets using medical-tourism fronts (no Central Asian involvement found):**
  - **Bangladesh–India racket:** an Indraprastha Apollo surgeon was arrested. A medical-tourism firm ("Al Shifa") handled donor and recipient testing. Donors were paid about Rs 4–5 lakh and recipients charged Rs 25–30 lakh — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/doctors/bangladesh-india-kidney-transplant-racket-indraprastha-apollo-surgeon-held-for-running-racket-at-yatharth-hospital-131358) (2024)
  - **Kerala:** a racket trafficked donors to Iran "under the guise of medical tourism"; the NIA arrested a man at Kochi airport, who admitted moving about 20 people. A search summary dates the arrest to Nov 2025, but that is unverified and the case may be from 2024 — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/states/south/kerala/organ-trade-under-guise-of-medical-tourism-5-year-long-racket-busted-in-kerala-247456); [IndToday](https://indtoday.com/organ-trafficking-scam-links-telangana-kerala-and-iran/)
  - **Al Jazeera (July 2025):** enforcement is sporadic and in tension with promoting a medical-tourism sector worth $7.6bn in 2024 — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2025/7/4/village-of-one-kidney-india-bangladesh-organ-traffickers-rob-poor-donors)
- **Precarious broker labour:** Russian-speaking interpreter-brokers in Delhi work under "strenuous conditions in volatile healthcare markets" — [SOAS CALA 2019](https://glocal.soas.ac.uk/cala2019-230/) **[pre-2024]**

### Inferences
- The reputational baseline for intermediaries in the CIS is low, and Kazakhstan's MoH is publicly warning patients about them (2026). Verifiable credentials help a new entrant here: dual medical licences, a named physician, a registered legal entity in KZ, and written contracts.
