# MBBS-Abroad Marketplace Players, Agent Business Models and Market Size (India source market, as of Oct 2026)

Research notes for a proposed app that is free for students. Agents pay a monthly subscription plus a fee per lead that views their contact, and agents and universities can buy ads. Research date: 14 Oct 2026. Items dated before 2024 are marked **[pre-2024]**.

Method caveat: WebFetch could not resolve many primary domains (outlookindia, medianama, mea.gov.in, careers360, medicaldialogues, eurasianet, thepienews, azimpremjiuniversity, indembastana). Some findings below therefore rest on search-engine extracts of those pages, not full reads. Where that matters, the finding says so.

## 1. Generic Indian education marketplaces and lead-gen platforms: MBBS-abroad coverage, revenue models, prices, scale, criticisms

### Takeaway
India's large college-discovery portals (Shiksha, Collegedunia, CollegeDekho, Careers360) earn most of their money by selling student leads to colleges on a cost-per-lead (CPL) or cost-per-admission basis, plus banner advertising. All of them publish MBBS-abroad content that captures leads. None found runs a transparent, verified-agent marketplace for MBBS abroad. Study-abroad counselling firms (Leverage Edu, Leap Scholar, Yocket, upGrad Abroad) mostly serve Anglophone and European destinations. They earn university commissions and pay for heavy advertising, and several still lose money. Shiksha's billings fell in Q4 FY26 because AI search cut its Google referral traffic. That weakens the SEO-to-lead-sale model these portals rely on.

**General study-abroad agent commission benchmarks (not MBBS-specific)**
- Agents are typically paid by the university on enrolment, at about 10–15% of first-year tuition and up to 25% for shorter programmes (per a WES sector overview cited in the source). This creates conflicts of interest — [GradPilot](https://gradpilot.com/news/study-abroad-consultancy-agents-what-students-should-know)
- The industry standard is 10–20% of first-year tuition, usually calculated on net tuition after scholarships. Some pathway providers pay flat fees of $500–$1,500 per enrolled student — [AGL, 2026](https://agl.ac/study-abroad-agent-commission-structures/)
- UK universities paid an average of £1,767 in agent commission per non-EU recruit (undated) — [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/uk-universities-paid-average-1767-agents-commission-per-feng)
- Indian agents have alleged that some UK university recruitment managers ask them for cash or a share of their commission — [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/university-recruiters-indian-agents/)

- None of the generic platforms appears to charge MBBS **agents** a transparent subscription plus per-view fee. Their buyers are mainly colleges and universities, and MBBS-abroad agents mostly buy ads or leads informally. A student-free, agent-pays marketplace would therefore be a new pricing model in this niche. It would still compete for the same NEET-aspirant traffic that these portals already capture through SEO.
- Shiksha's AI-search problem is a warning for any model that depends on Google traffic. App-based, community and YouTube channels may be more durable acquisition routes.
- CollegeDekho spends roughly Rs 126 crore on advertising against Rs 222 crore of revenue. Buying student traffic in Indian edtech is expensive, which is why a free-for-students app's economics hinge on organic acquisition.

- No confirmed full-year FY26 Shiksha revenue figure (only Q1 revenue and Q4 billings).
- I could not verify whether upGrad Abroad, IDP, ApplyBoard or Studyportals offer MBBS placements in Russia or Central Asia. No sources surfaced either way.
- No Leap Scholar FY25 financials were found.
- I did not find AmbitionBox or Glassdoor material on lead-selling practices; only one MouthShut review.

## 2. MBBS-abroad specific platforms and big agents: who they are, how they make money, what they charge

### Takeaway
The MBBS-abroad agent market in India is highly fragmented. There are hundreds of consultancies, many run by former foreign medical graduates, and all of them claim to be "official representatives" or "No. 1." I found no independent ranking or market-share data. The dominant revenue source is commission or contractor margin from foreign universities, often bundled with hostel, food and fee-collection services. On top of that come "service/processing" charges to students. Public, MBBS-specific commission figures are essentially unavailable. Regulators (NMC) and Indian embassies repeatedly flag agent malpractice, and police cases involve losses of lakhs per student.

### Cited Findings
- **Rus Education:** described as "a leading organisation" sending Indian students to Russia, headed in India by Sayed Kamruzzaman, who noted that up to 90% of Indian students in Russia study at medical institutions. The article is undated and likely **[pre-2024]** — [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/world/russian-universities-woo-indian-students-24661.html); its current site markets MBBS in multiple countries — [Rus Education](https://www.ruseducation.in/mbbs-in-abroad/)
- Russian universities run joint promotional events in India. In 2023, Tula State University reported about 30,000 Indian students studying in Russia, with medicine especially in demand — [Tula State University, Oct 2023](https://tulsu.ru/en/news/1411) **[pre-2024]**. In March 2025, a meeting in New Delhi on Indian students in Russian medical universities included India's NMC chairman — [Mordovia State University](https://mrsu.ru/en/university/news/strengthening-cooperation-in-medical-education/)
- A search extract said a Russian Consulate General event named "Study Abroad Educational Consultants" as the authorised Indian representative for Russian universities. I could not confirm which article said this; it surfaced alongside [Medical Dialogues: Russian varsities offer 500 medical seats](https://education.medicaldialogues.in/russian-varsities-offer-500-medical-seats-to-indian-students) and [EducationWorld](https://educationworld.in/?p=155795). - **Global Medical Foundation (GMF)** runs an MBBS-abroad admissions site, including Ukraine pages — [gmfadmission.in](https://gmfadmission.in/mbbs-in-ukraine/)

This is self-description — [Collegedunia listing](https://s3.collegedunia.com/institute/7537-abc-studyv-links-study-abroad-consultant-new-delhi)
- Agents are often former foreign medical graduates. Per a PTI story, three siblings who failed FMGE became MBBS-abroad consultants. The same report says ~25,000 Indian students go abroad for MBBS each year and gives FMGE pass rates of 25.79% (2019), 14.68% (2020), 23.83% (2021), 39% (2022) and 10.6% (2023) — [Deccan Herald / PTI](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/failing-to-get-licence-to-practice-as-doctors-3-siblings-turn-consultants-for-mbbs-aspirants-2814581); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/3-siblings-turn-consultants-for-mbbs-aspirants-after-failing-crack-fmge)

### Inferences
- The market splits into roughly four agent types:
  1. Country-specialist "contractors" or official representatives with university agreements. These often also run hostels and mess and collect fees, e.g. Rus Education in Russia.
  2. Multi-country MBBS consultancies such as GMF, OM, RMC, Eklavya Overseas and Rus Education's multi-country arm.
  3. Sub-agents, coaching-centre tie-ups and senior students who feed the contractors.
  4. YouTube and Instagram influencer-consultants.

  A marketplace would mostly be selling to types 2–4, since type 1 already has captive supply.
- Agent revenue per student plausibly comes from university commission or contractor margin plus student service fees. Lakh-scale fraud losses (₹3.5–6 lakh per victim) suggest that students are used to paying agents several lakh rupees. That would make a per-lead fee of a few hundred to a few thousand rupees affordable for agents, though this is not verified.
- Marketing like "pay fees directly to the university" and "no donation" shows that trust is the core unmet need. A neutral platform could make fee transparency (itemised fees, direct-to-university payment) a listing requirement.

### Gaps
- **No reliable MBBS-specific figure was found for university-to-agent commission** (e.g. $500–$3,000 per student or % of tuition) for Russia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan or Georgia. Only general study-abroad benchmarks exist (10–20% of first-year tuition; $500–1,500 flat). This needs primary interviews or agent contracts.
- **No reliable data on standard service or processing fees** charged to students by MBBS consultancies. The ₹50k–₹3 lakh range in the brief could not be verified.
- I could not verify the existence, scale or models of the specific names in the brief: Kings Education, MBBS Bhavan, Edufever, Aspiring Doctors, MBBS Lighthouse, Navigator, Medlink. No sources surfaced on them in my searches, and I make no claim about them.
- No market-share or student-volume ranking of MBBS agents exists in public sources.
- No Reddit (r/NEET, r/MBBS) material was retrievable.

## 3. How foreign medical universities (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Russia, Georgia, etc.) recruit Indian students

### Takeaway
Central Asian and Russian medical universities mainly recruit through appointed "contractors" or authorised representatives. These intermediaries often handle hostel, welfare and even fee collection, and they run networks of sub-agents in India, including senior Indian students. Some universities, such as Kazakh National Medical University, publish lists of cooperating companies. Others also allow direct online applications. Indian embassies and the NMC repeatedly warn about misleading sub-agents, admissions over capacity and diploma-mill behaviour.

### Cited Findings
- The Embassy of India in Astana's student advisory says Kazakh medical universities appoint "contractors" and often insist on that channel. Universities "prefer to work through the 'contractors' for provision of hostel facilities, general welfare of the students, and even collecting fee." These "contractors have their sub-agents in India," and "in many cases, senior Indian students themselves function as contractors and agents." Students are told to "verify from the authorised contractor of the university before making any payment to sub-agents in India." Contractors and consultancy agencies in India "may not have updated knowledge and may [make] unrealistic promises and give outdated/misleading information." This is a search extract; the advisory's date is unconfirmed — [Embassy of India Astana, Student Advisory](https://www.indembastana.gov.in/content/Student-advisory-Kazakhstan.pdf); [Embassy students page](https://indembastana.gov.in/page/students)
- Kazakh National Medical University: applicants must apply through companies that cooperate with the university, whose list and contacts are posted on the university website (secondary source) — [Admistay](https://admistay.com/admission/kazakhstan/almaty/university/kazakh-national-medical-university-3771)
- Kazakhstan-Resey Medical University accepts applications either online through its official site or through an authorised representative, so direct channels exist at some schools — [Times of College](https://timesofcollege.com/abroad/kazakhstan-resey-medical-university-206665+admissions)
- An Azim Premji University study says medical colleges in Central and East Asia have agreements with private contractors to bring in foreign students. Those contractors have sub-agents across India, and the intermediaries "may not reveal all relevant information to attract more students" (search extract) — [Azim Premji University](https://azimpremjiuniversity.edu.in/insights-from-social-context/indian-student-migration-for-medical-education-to-central-and-east-asia-understanding-demand-supply-and-roles-of-market-and-state)
- Per Indian embassy data cited in a research paper, Kazakhstan has 11 "authorized" medical universities, Kyrgyzstan 12, Uzbekistan 11 and Tajikistan 1 — [Eurasian Research Institute](https://eurasian-research.org/publications/perspectives-of-central-asia-for-indian-students)
- Embassy of India Bishkek's medical-course advisory (updated 15 July 2025) says Kyrgyz authorities opened a criminal case against International Medical University (Bishkek) staff for issuing medical diplomas to foreign students who were not actually present. It also warns students not to rely on the old MCI list — [Embassy of India Bishkek](https://indembbishkek.gov.in/page.php?pageid=47)
- In Uzbekistan, the Indian Embassy in Tashkent flagged students being admitted beyond the institute's intake capacity and teaching not being in English, and NMC cited agent malpractice (2026) — [Careers360 News](https://news.careers360.com/mbbs-abroad-nmc-warning-3-uzbekistan-medical-college-admission-tsmu-bangalore-campus-samarkand-state-university-bukhara-fees-neet)
- Some Uzbek universities have opened offshore or partner campuses in India; the TSMU Bengaluru campus was flagged by NMC in 2026 — [Careers360 News](https://news.careers360.com/mbbs-abroad-nmc-warning-3-uzbekistan-medical-college-admission-tsmu-bangalore-campus-samarkand-state-university-bukhara-fees-neet)
- Russia uses government-to-government and university outreach. Russian universities hold education events in India and met NMC in New Delhi in March 2025 — [Mordovia State University](https://mrsu.ru/en/university/news/strengthening-cooperation-in-medical-education/); [Tula State University 2023](https://tulsu.ru/en/news/1411)
- Embassy guidance repeated in consultancy content: for Kazakhstan, the Indian Embassy recommends students pay fees to the university themselves and get a proper receipt rather than paying through recruitment agents — [Eurasian Research Institute / related guides](https://eurasian-research.org/publications/perspectives-of-central-asia-for-indian-students); [Careers360: Study MBBS in Kazakhstan](https://studyabroad.careers360.com/articles/study-mbbs-in-kazakhstan)

### Inferences
- Universities in this corridor usually outsource recruitment and student services to a small number of contractors per university, sometimes exclusive ones. This "official representative" structure means universities already have a sales channel. Their willingness to pay for marketplace ads may depend on whether they are under-enrolled or trying to bypass contractors.
- The multi-tier chain (university → contractor → sub-agent → student) is the root of opacity and fee padding. An app that shows each agent's tier (official contractor vs sub-agent), backed by the university's published partner lists, would address a gap the embassies themselves highlight.
- Exclusivity matters for the marketplace. If a university has one official contractor, other agents listing that university are sub-agents by definition, and the platform should label them as such.

### Gaps
- No published commission or contractor-margin terms for any Kazakh, Uzbek, Kyrgyz, Russian or Georgian university were found.
- Georgia's recruitment channels (agents vs direct) were not documented in the sources found.
- I could not confirm whether universities in the Philippines, Nepal, Bangladesh or Egypt use similar contractor systems.
- The date of the Astana embassy advisory was not confirmed.

## 4. Analogous Indian marketplaces that charge service providers per lead or subscription (Justdial, IndiaMART, Sulekha, Practo, 99acres/Housing, Urban Company): pricing, lead-quality handling, lessons

### Takeaway
Indian lead marketplaces mostly sell annual or monthly subscription packages, sometimes with "assured" lead tiers, and charge premiums for featured placement. Average ticket sizes run from a few thousand rupees per campaign (Justdial) to about ₹60–67k per year (IndiaMART). All face recurring complaints about fake, duplicate or resold leads, auto-debits that are hard to cancel, and sales pressure. Medical platforms (Practo) have drawn regulator attention for paid placement. The lessons for an MBBS agent marketplace are: verify leads, publish lead-exclusivity rules, offer refunds for invalid leads, keep sponsored and organic results visibly separate, and avoid aggressive recurring billing.

### Cited Findings
- **Justdial:** average realisation per paid campaign was ₹4,742 in Q1 FY25, with ~591.7K paid campaigns — [ICICI Direct, July 2024](https://icicidirect.com/mailimages/IDirect_JustDial_CoUpdate_July2024.pdf). Tier-I city pricing is almost double Tier-II, and management saw room for annual pricing to rise toward ₹18,000–20,000 **[pre-2024, Apr 2023]** — [ICICI Direct, Apr 2023](https://www.icicidirect.com/mailimages/IDirect_JustDial_CoUpdate_Apr23.pdf)
- Justdial moved from flat listing fees to an ad-led model with large premiums for featured positions, and later to pay-per-lead **[pre-2024, old profile]** — [Venture Intelligence blog](https://blog.ventureintelligence.com/?p=2731)
- A lead-gen vendor estimates Justdial "Assured" leads at ₹400–800 per lead in tier-2 cities (vendor claim) — [QuickEstimate, 2026](https://quickestimate.co/blog/solar-lead-from-justdial)
- Complaints about Justdial include poor lead quality, zero conversion, unresponsive support and continued ECS auto-debits — [ConsumerComplaints.in](https://www.consumercomplaints.in/justdial-com-complaint-against-justdial-for-poor-lead-quality-zero-business-conversion-unresponsive-support-and-continued-ecs-deductions-d-c3543240); [Voxya, Apr 2026](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/false-advertisement-and-looted/263016). One older review alleges a salesperson sold a fixed per-lead contract and the same leads went to many businesses **[pre-2024]** — [IndiaStudyChannel](https://www.indiastudychannel.com/resources/91861-Review-fraud-company-Justdial-com.aspx)
- SBI Securities (Nov 2025) lists slow growth in Justdial paid campaigns as a key risk, a sign of advertiser retention issues — [SBI Securities](https://www.sbisecurities.in/fileserver/research/reports/Pick%20of%20the%20Week_Just%20Dial%20Ltd.pdf)
- **IndiaMART:** about 2.22 lakh paying suppliers at end-FY26. Q4 saw a net decline of ~1,200 subscribers, attributed to a price hike in Silver plans — [IIFL](https://live-next.indiainfoline.com/news/markets/indiamart-structural-story-intact-despite-near-term-blip). Annualised revenue per paying supplier was about ₹67,000 in Q3 FY26 (+6% YoY), and Gold and Platinum customers contribute over 75% of revenue — [AlphaStreet](https://alphastreet.com/india/india-mart-q2-fy26-earnings-results); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/indiamart-q4-net-profit-plunges-72-yoy-to-%E2%82%B950-cr/)
- **Sulekha:** uses tiered subscription plans where better plans unlock more and better leads — [LeadSquared](https://www.leadsquared.com/learn/sales/lead-generation-websites-in-india-for-local-businesses/). One user who paid for a year-long subscription complained of "useless leads" **[pre-2024, ~2010]** — same source
- **Practo:** earns from doctors and clinics, not patients, through SaaS subscriptions and per-patient or sponsored charges — [FutureX](https://futurex.substack.com/p/what-went-wrong-with-practo). Sponsored listings appear at the top, while basic listing is free **[pre-2024, 2019]** — [HuffPost India](https://www.huffingtonpost.in/entry/medical-data-practo-privacy-spying_in_5cd2e1aee4b0a7dffccfa40d)
- The Tamil Nadu Medical Council sent notices to doctors in the top Practo search results, asking why they had advertised on the platform. Medical-ethics rules can constrain paid promotion **[pre-2024]** — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/tamil-nadu-medical-council-sends-out-more-notices-for-advertising)
- Practo's founder said no one can pay to boost organic rankings — [FutureX](https://futurex.substack.com/p/what-went-wrong-with-practo). A doctor reported paying ₹14,000 for a Practo subscription in 2020, having the account blocked and getting no refund (single review) — [Trustpilot](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/practo.com)
- **99acres / Housing.com:** both use broker subscription models plus project ad campaigns. 99acres claimed a 600+ person team that physically verifies properties, and Housing.com claimed all its listings were verified with real photos **[pre-2024, 2015]** — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/real-estate/story/realty-websites-99acres-housingcom-magicbricks-pros-cons-48034-2015-06-25)
- Recent user reviews of both allege that paid plans produced mostly fake enquiries or enquiries from other brokers, that lead data was withheld after plans expired, and that upgrade calls were relentless — [Trustpilot 99acres](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/99acres.com); [Trustpilot Housing.com](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/housing.com)

### Inferences
- **Pricing anchors for the proposed app.** Indian SMB-type service providers are used to roughly ₹5k–₹20k per year (Justdial-like) up to about ₹60–70k per year (IndiaMART premium tiers). An MBBS agent earns a far larger margin per student (lakhs, per Section 2), so a monthly subscription in the ₹5k–₹25k range plus ₹200–₹2,000 per contact view seems economically tolerable. This is an inference from analogues and is not validated.
- **Lessons:**
  1. Selling the same lead to many buyers is the top complaint. Cap the number of agents per lead (e.g. 3–5), disclose the cap to both sides, and refund leads that turn out invalid (fake number, wrong NEET status).
  2. Price hikes on entry tiers cause churn (IndiaMART Silver), so keep entry plans cheap and monetise through per-lead fees.
  3. Auto-debit complaints damage the brand (Justdial ECS), so allow easy cancellation.
  4. Sponsored placement must be clearly labelled and kept out of "verified" rankings (Practo precedent).
  5. Physical or document verification badges were a differentiator for 99acres and Housing, but have to be real to be credible.
- The "fee per student lead who views their contact" structure resembles a reverse of Justdial's pay-per-lead: the student initiates the reveal. That is stronger on consent and intent, and would likely reduce fake-lead disputes.

### Gaps
- No current official price lists were found for Justdial, Sulekha, 99acres, Housing.com or Practo.
- No Urban Company data was gathered (commission rates, lead charges to partners).
- I could not find formal published lead-dispute or refund policies for any of these platforms.

## 5. Existing "verified agent directory" or neutral comparison platform for MBBS abroad

### Takeaway
I found no trusted, neutral, verified-agent directory or rating platform for MBBS-abroad consultants in India. The closest things are unclaimed business directories, consultancy-run "top 10 consultants" listicles, and official sources that verify **universities** but not agents: the NMC recognised-institution list or FMGL criteria, the WHO World Directory of Medical Schools, and embassy advisories. A few universities publish their own lists of cooperating companies. This is a clear gap.

### Cited Findings
- Directory sites such as Ternrise list MBBS consultancies, but listings can be unclaimed. They publish only factual business information and internal summaries, with no ratings — [Ternrise](https://ternrise.com/firms/educational-consultant-jaipur-india-9)
- "Best MBBS abroad consultancy" rankings are written by consultancies or study-abroad firms themselves, e.g. [Ambitio: 7 Best Consultancy Firms for MBBS Abroad](https://ambitio.club/blog/best-consultancy-for-mbbs-abroad/), [OM Consultants: Top MBBS Abroad Consultants](https://www.omedu.com/mbbs-abroad-consultants/), [Ensure Education: Best MBBS Abroad Consultancy 2026](https://www.ensureeducation.com/index.php/blog/best-mbbs-abroad-consultancy-in-india-2026-complete-guide-for-indian-students), [futureMBBS](https://future-mbbs.com/best-consultancy-for-mbbs-abroad/)
- Forum and Q&A threads (e.g. DigitalOcean community, assorted forums) carry agent self-promotion with no verification method — [DigitalOcean Community](https://www.digitalocean.com/community/questions/which-is-the-best-mbbs-abroad-education-consultants-in-india)
- NMC verifies institutions, not agents. One 2026 blog claims the NMC website lists 1,178 approved foreign medical institutions as of March 2026 — [iamBBS blog](https://www.iambbs.com/blog/mbbs-abroad-scam-alert-2026-how-to-identify-fake-agents-and). This is contradicted by a consultancy guide stating that NMC does not publish or endorse a blanket list of "NMC-approved foreign medical universities" — [FindUrCollege](https://findurcollege.com/mbbs-abroad). Treat the 1,178 figure as unverified.
- Embassy advisories (Astana, Bishkek) tell students to verify agents with the university's authorised contractor and to pay the university directly. In effect they push verification onto the student — [Embassy of India Astana](https://www.indembastana.gov.in/content/Student-advisory-Kazakhstan.pdf); [Embassy of India Bishkek](https://indembbishkek.gov.in/page.php?pageid=47)
- Some universities publish lists of authorised partner companies, e.g. KazNMU — [Admistay](https://admistay.com/admission/kazakhstan/almaty/university/kazakh-national-medical-university-3771)
- Careers360 publishes anti-fraud and NMC-blacklist content but does not rate agents — [Careers360](https://studyabroad.careers360.com/articles/beware-of-fraudsters-while-planning-study-mbbs-abroad)

### Inferences
- **The main gap for the proposed app is a verified agent layer.** Verification could include GST and company registration checks; university-issued authorisation letters cross-checked against university partner lists; agent tier labels (official contractor vs sub-agent); NMC/FMGL-compliance tags on each listed university; student-verified reviews tied to enrolment proof; and fee-transparency badges (itemised fees, direct-to-university payment).
- Neutrality conflicts with the ad model. If universities and agents buy ads, the platform has to separate paid placement from verified rankings (see the Practo lesson) or lose student trust, and trust is the main reason a student would use it over Google or YouTube.
- Incumbent portals (Careers360, Shiksha) already own MBBS-abroad SEO content but have not built agent verification. That suggests either the economics are unattractive at their scale, or it is an open niche. Shiksha's AI-search revenue pressure may push it toward new offerings (per its stated pivot), so it could become a fast follower.

### Gaps
- I did not find any government or industry-body registry of overseas-education agents in India for MBBS, nor any accreditation scheme specific to MBBS agents.
- I did not research how widely agents in this vertical hold global agent-quality certifications (e.g. ICEF, AIRC, British Council agent training).
- I did not look at the Google review profiles of major MBBS consultancies, so trust levels for existing names are unquantified.

## 6. Market size: Indian students going abroad for MBBS each year, destinations, and spend (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Roughly 20,000–25,000 Indian students start MBBS abroad each year (PTI and consultancy estimates; there is no official annual intake figure). About 80,000–110,000 are enrolled at any time across the main destinations. MEA data as of 1 Jan 2025 shows about 27,000 Indian university students in Russia, 16,500 in Kyrgyzstan, 16,000 in Georgia, about 9,000–10,000 each in Kazakhstan, the Philippines and Bangladesh, and 7,500 in Uzbekistan, which reportedly grew past 10,000 in 2025. The 2025 FMGE sessions had about 80,000 sittings combined. Demand is driven by ~22–23 lakh NEET registrants competing for ~1.18 lakh Indian MBBS seats.

### Cited Findings
- **MEA "Data on Indian Students Abroad (as on January 1, 2025)"** gives university-level counts of Russia ~27,000 (27,222 including schools), Kyrgyzstan 16,500, Georgia 16,000, Kazakhstan 9,064, Philippines 9,500, Bangladesh 9,862, Uzbekistan 7,500, Nepal 2,200, Tajikistan 1,234 and Egypt 1,500. These cover all disciplines, not only medicine, and come from a search extract of the PDF — [MEA Annexure I](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/CPV/557-en-01-04-12-2025.pdf)
- MEA data tabled in Parliament counts 18.82 lakh Indian students abroad in 2025, 12.54 lakh of them in higher education, down from 13.3 lakh in 2024. That was the first fall after three years of growth. Russia, Kyrgyzstan and Georgia are noted as rising low-cost STEM and medical destinations — [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/outbound-indian-university-enrolments-fall-after-three-year-rise/); [ICEF Monitor, Dec 2025](https://monitor.icef.com/2025/12/the-number-of-indian-students-abroad-fell-in-2025/)
- Indian students heading to the US, UK and Canada fell sharply, while Russia and Bangladesh gained (July 2025) — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/nri/study/story/india-sees-sharp-fall-in-students-heading-to-us-uk-canada-russia-and-bangladesh-gain-486434-2025-07-26). This source reports Bangladesh at 29,232 Indian students in 2024 (all levels) versus 20,368 in 2023, which conflicts with the MEA university figure of 9,862.
- Uzbekistan: Indian students nearly tripled to more than 10,000, mostly in medicine, up from about 3,500 the year before (June 2025) — [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/uzbekistan-sees-three-fold-rise-in-indian-students/). Uzbek statistics put the 2023/24 figure at 5,733 — [Kun.uz](https://monitor.kun.uz/en/news/2024/11/01/uzbekistans-universities-see-growing-international-enrollment-led-by-south-asian-students)
- Kyrgyzstan: about 17,000 Indian students as of May 2024, mostly in Bishkek — [Embassy of India Bishkek press release, 22 May 2024](https://indembbishkek.gov.in/newsImage/1716399515_Press-Release-22.5.24.pdf). Embassy data for 2023 cited over 17,400 in Kyrgyzstan and over 9,500 in Kazakhstan — [Eurasian Research Institute](https://www.eurasian-research.org/?p=42137)
- Russia: about 30,000 Indian students in Oct 2023, per Russian officials — [Tula State University](https://tulsu.ru/en/news/1411) **[pre-2024]**
- Ukraine (post-war): nearly 1,100 Indian medical students were still in Ukraine per the Government of India (date not confirmed) — [Careers360 News](https://news.careers360.com/nearly-1100-indian-students-currently-in-ukraine-government/amp)
- Annual flow: "around 25,000 Indian students go abroad to study MBBS" every year (PTI) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/failing-to-get-licence-to-practice-as-doctors-3-siblings-turn-consultants-for-mbbs-aspirants-2814581). Consultancy and blog estimates range from 20,000 to 25,000 — [Shiksha](https://www.shiksha.com/studyabroad/best-countries-to-study-mbbs-abroad-articlepage-2223); [Leap Scholar](https://leapscholar.com/blog/best-countries-for-mbbs-for-indian-students/)
- **FMGE (a proxy for graduate output):** December 2025 had 43,933 candidates appearing and 10,264 passing (~23%) — [Allen](https://news.allen.in/fmge-2025-results-released-77-percent-fail/); [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/education/medical-admissions/only-10264-candidates-clear-fmge-december-2025-over-77-percent-fail-screening-test-163962). June 2025 had 36,039 appearing and 6,707 passing (18.61%) — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/fmge-2025-results-out-just-18-percent-pass-over-29000-fail-the-screening-test)
- **Demand driver (NEET-UG 2025):** about 22.7–23.4 lakh registrations and ~20.9 lakh appeared; 12,36,531 qualified; 1,18,190 MBBS seats in India (MoHFW, April 2025) — [Careers360 News](https://news.careers360.com/mcc-neet-ug-counselling-2025-begins-tomorrow-registration-colleges-fees-link-12-36-lakh-compete-for-1-18-lakh-mbbs-seats/amp); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/over-12-lakh-qualify-neet-ug-2025-enn25062402458)
- The NMC FMGL Regulations 2021 require course duration, English medium, curriculum, clinical training and internship to match Indian standards; non-compliant degrees disqualify graduates from practising in India — [Careers360 News](https://news.careers360.com/nmc-blacklists-4-foreign-medical-colleges-in-belize-uzbekistan-america-advisory-harassment-indian-students-mbbs-fees/amp)

### Inferences
- **Stock across the main MBBS destinations** (Russia, Kyrgyzstan, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Philippines, Bangladesh, Nepal, Tajikistan, Egypt) is about 99,000 university students per MEA's Jan 2025 table, most of them in medicine. With 5.5–6-year programmes, that implies an annual intake of about 17,000–20,000. Including China, Iran and other destinations brings it to the 20,000–25,000 the PTI figure cites.
- **Lead funnel for the app:** about 11 lakh NEET-qualified students do not get an Indian MBBS seat (12.4 lakh qualified minus 1.18 lakh seats). That is the top of the funnel, of whom roughly 2% actually go abroad. Agents are therefore paying to find the few percent of high-intent families, which is why lead quality and intent signals (NEET score, budget, passport status) matter.
- **Spend sizing (rough and unverified):** if 20,000–25,000 students a year each spend ₹20–40 lakh on a full programme (a range common in consultancy materials but not verified from an authoritative source), annual cohort spend is ~₹4,000–10,000 crore. If agents earned even 5–10% of that through commission plus fees, the agent revenue pool would be ~₹200–1,000 crore a year. This is an order-of-magnitude estimate only.
- **Growth and mix shift:** Uzbekistan's fast rise, Russia and Bangladesh gaining while Anglophone destinations fall, and NMC crackdowns on specific universities (Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan) together create demand for up-to-date, neutral university-compliance information.

### Gaps
- No official annual count of new Indian MBBS-abroad entrants was found. A Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha reply on NMC eligibility certificates would be the best source but was not located.
- The MEA figures are for all disciplines; no medicine-only breakdown by country was found.
- Georgia's medicine-only count and the Philippines' post-2024 trend were not verified.
- No authoritative total tuition or spend figure for Indian MBBS-abroad students was found; the ₹20–40 lakh per-student programme cost comes from consultancy materials.
