## Q1. India, doctor ethics: can an Indian-registered doctor take commission or referral fees from hospitals, directly or through their own facilitator company?

### Takeaway
Probably not, and the risk is high. The binding code in India is still the 2002 IMC Ethics Regulations. The NMC's 2023 replacement was put in abeyance in August 2023. Clause 6.4 of the 2002 code bars a doctor from receiving any "commission or bonus" for referring patients, and from taking part "directly or indirectly" in fee-splitting or rebating. On 6 Oct 2026 the NMC issued an advertising and communication notice that reportedly also bans "referral fees or lead-generation fees linked to patient referrals". A company owned by the doctor is unlikely to protect them. The ban applies to doctors, not to hospitals or non-doctor companies, which is why non-doctor-owned structures exist.

### Cited Findings
**Status of the codes (CONFIRMED)**
- The NMC notified the Registered Medical Practitioner (Professional Conduct) Regulations, 2023 on 2 Aug 2023. A gazette notification dated 23 Aug 2023 then said they would "not be operative" until a further NMC gazette notification. The NMC brought the IMC (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 back into effect under the NMC Act, 2019. — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/health-news/nmc/breaking-news-nmc-puts-its-controversial-registered-medical-practitioner-professional-conduct-regulations-2023-put-on-hold-116314); [BW Healthcare World](https://www.bwhealthcareworld.com/article/nmc-puts-new-regulations-on-docs-in-abeyance-directs-to-follow-old-guidelines-from-2002-488890); [Complinity](https://complinity.com/legal-update/national-medical-commission-registered-medical-practitioner-professional-conduct-regulations-2023-not-to-be-operative-and-effective-till-further-notification-10477/); [Legality Simplified](https://www.legalitysimplified.com/nmc-keeps-its-registered-medical-practitioner-professional-conduct-regulations-2023-in-abeyance/)
- An amendment, the RMP (Professional Conduct) (Amendment) Regulations, 2023, was notified on 24 Aug 2023. — [TeamLease RegTech](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/25986/national-medical-commission-registered-medical-practitioners-professio/)
- 2026 compliance guides say the 2002 Regulations remain the operative code and the 2023 Regulations remain in abeyance. These are secondary sources. — [DoubleSure 2026 guide](https://www.doublesure.health/medical-advertising-compliance-india); [iChelon Consulting](https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/nmc-2026-guide-for-doctors)
- The suspended 2023 Regulations had made the ban on commissions for referrals stricter than in 2002. — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/nmc-registered-medical-practitioner-professional-conduct-regulations-2023)
- The NMC's 2022 draft would have barred doctors and their families from accepting gifts, hospitality, cash, consultancy fees or honoraria from "commercial healthcare establishments… or corporate hospitals". — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/news/new-norms-for-doctors-proposed-nmc-draft-rules); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/new-regulations-on-professional-conduct-of-doctors-proposed-1112134.html)

**What clause 6.4 of the 2002 code prohibits (CONFIRMED in substance; exact wording should be checked against the gazette)**
- The 2002 Regulations bar registered practitioners from giving, soliciting or receiving any gift, gratuity, commission or bonus for referring, recommending or procuring a patient for treatment. A separate limb bars a doctor from taking part "directly or indirectly" in dividing, rebating, splitting or refunding fees. The rules bind registered medical practitioners only, not pharma companies, hospitals or digital health platforms. — [Conventus Law, "Rx for referrals"](https://conventuslaw.com/report/rx-for-referrals-navigating-the-ethical-considerations-in-indias-medical-landscape/)
- Clause 6.4.2 extends the ban to referring specimens or material for diagnostics, with an exception for salaries paid to qualified staff. — search summary of [Delhi Medical Council resolution](https://www.delhimedicalcouncil.org/pdf/date.pdf/24.7.2014.doc) and related results

**The NMC notice of 6 Oct 2026 (REPORTED, very recent; primary text not seen)**
- The NMC's Ethics and Medical Registration Board issued "ethical advertising and public communication" guidelines for doctors and hospitals by public notice dated 06.10.2026, effective immediately. Secondary sources give the reference as R-13014/01/2024-Ethics. The notice reportedly bans patient-soliciting advertising, testimonials, and before-and-after photos. It also reportedly bans doctors from offering, taking or sharing "commissions, rebates, bonuses, gifts, referral fees or lead-generation fees linked to patient referrals", and requires the doctor's SMR/NMR registration number to appear in public posts. Reported penalties escalate: a warning, then censure and a fine, then suspension of 3–6 months. Serious breaches reportedly bring 6–12 months' suspension, and repeated breaches 1–3 years off the register. Appeals go to the EMRB within 60 days. — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/health-news/nmc/nmc-issues-ethical-advertising-public-communication-guidelines-for-doctors-hospitals-details-180731); [iChelon](https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/nmc-2026-guide-for-doctors); [Medvyn](https://medvyn.in/nmc-ethical-advertising-guidelines-hospitals-doctors/); [GoNexaflow](https://www.gonexaflow.com/blog/latest-nmc-advertising-guidelines-2026); [Public Media Solution](https://publicmediasolution.com/blog/nmc-ethical-advertising-guidelines-doctors-hospitals/). Caveat: these are mainly consultancy and blog summaries. One secondary source describes a new "NMC 2026 Code of Ethics", but other sources contradict it — [iChelon "2026 ethics code"](https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/nmc-2026-ethics-code-complete-guide-doctors). Treat as UNCERTAIN until the NMC notice is read.

**Enforcement and tax**
- After a sting operation, the Delhi Medical Council told doctors to "refrain from accepting or offering rebates and commission, directly or indirectly" in relation to patient treatment, and warned of disciplinary action. — [Delhi Medical Council](https://www.delhimedicalcouncil.org/pdf/date.pdf/24.7.2014.doc)
- A Cobrapost sting alleged a "thriving commission racket in 20 top hospitals". — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/cobrapost-unearths-a-thriving-commission-racket-in-20-top-hospitals/51518.html)
- An MCI press release noted that institutions running high-cost investigations and treatment "offer inducement to the doctor who referred the patient". — [Indian Journal of Medical Ethics PDF](https://www.issuesinmedicalethics.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/528-5.pdf)
- The Telangana State Medical Council issued a notice to a diagnostic centre over kickbacks paid for referrals. — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/telangana-state-medical-council-issues-notice-to-diagnostic-center-on-kickbacks)
- Recent council discipline has involved industry inducements, not medical-tourism referrals. In July 2026 the Gujarat Medical Council suspended three dermatologists for 13 months over a pharma-sponsored Europe trip — [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/gallery/india/gujarat-doctors-suspension-medical-council-suspends-three-dermatologists-for-13-months-over-pharma-sponsored-europe-trip-evnhzb2). In Sept 2025 the Telangana council suspended five doctors — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/doctors/telangana-medical-council-suspends-5-doctors-for-ethical-violations-professional-misconduct-155961). The MCI suspended 15 doctors in Madhya Pradesh over sponsored foreign trips — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/mci-suspends-registrations-of-15-doctors-in-madhya-pradesh-115022301078_1.html).
- Discipline is handled by the State Medical Council where the doctor is registered. — [iChelon](https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/nmc-2026-guide-for-doctors)
- Tax angle: the Calcutta High Court in *Peerless Hospitex v PCIT* (2022) held that a hospital cannot deduct referral commission paid to doctors, relying on Explanation 1 to s.37(1), the 2012 CBDT circular and the MCI regulations. — [ITATonline](https://itatonline.org/digest/peerless-hospitex-hospital-and-research-centre-ltd-v-pcit-2022-447-itr-60-213-dtr-81-326-ctr-249-287-taxman-711-cal-hc/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/referral-fee-paid-to-doctors-for-referring-patients-not-deductible-hc-follows-apex-lab-ruling/). The Mumbai ITAT reached a similar result in *Stemade Biotech*. — [ITATonline](https://itatonline.org/digest/stemade-biotech-p-ltd-v-dcit-2022-195-itd-346-217-ttj-960-mum-trib)

### Inferences
- **Using a company as a wrapper is high risk.** Clause 6.4 covers taking part "directly or indirectly" in fee rebating or splitting, and the Delhi council's wording covers commissions received "directly or indirectly". So a facilitator company owned or controlled by the doctor, collecting per-patient commissions from hospitals, is likely to be treated as the doctor receiving them indirectly. The reported Oct 2026 ban on "lead-generation fees" closes the obvious argument that the company's fee is a marketing fee and not a referral fee.
- **Tax risk follows the payment.** Hospitals may become reluctant to pay a company owned by a doctor, because those payments risk the *Peerless* disallowance.
- **Structures used in the market, in rough order of lower to higher risk.** None has a cited regulatory ruling; a lawyer must confirm each.
  1. **Patient-paid fee.** The patient pays a transparent, flat service fee for logistics, interpretation, visas and accommodation. The fee does not depend on which hospital is chosen, and the hospital pays nothing.
  2. **Disclosed, fixed fee for services the hospital actually receives.** The hospital pays a fair-value, non-per-patient fee for services it really gets, such as an interpreter desk or translation of records. The arrangement is disclosed to patients in writing, and the doctor does not choose the hospital.
  3. **Non-doctor-owned facilitator with the doctor as salaried medical advisor.** The doctor holds no equity, and family-held equity is also risky as "indirect". The doctor draws no commission-linked pay and does not steer patients to the paying hospitals.
  4. **Per-patient commission to a doctor-owned company.** This is the most exposed model.
- **The founder's dual registration doubles the exposure.** Conduct in Kazakhstan can create Indian disciplinary exposure, because the State Medical Council has jurisdiction over its registrant. Kazakh ethics rules apply as well (see Q7).
- **Clinical advice is a separate issue.** If the founder gives clinical opinions while choosing hospitals, that is practising medicine, which brings licensing and liability questions in the country where the patient is.

### Gaps
- No published State Medical Council or NMC disciplinary decision was found that deals specifically with a doctor-owned medical tourism facilitator taking hospital commissions.
- The exact gazette wording of clause 6.4.1 and of the 6 Oct 2026 NMC notice was not read. It is unconfirmed whether the notice's commission ban is binding regulation or "guidelines".
- No source was found on whether the NMC asserts jurisdiction over a registrant's conduct abroad, such as referrals made from Kazakhstan.
- The Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 and their application to cross-border teleconsultations with foreign patients were not researched (search budget exhausted).

## Q2. India: rules for facilitators (Ministry of Tourism / MDA recognition, the National Medical & Wellness Tourism Board, Heal in India, NABH/JCI)

### Takeaway
India has no mandatory licence or registration for a medical tourism facilitator. What exists is voluntary:
- Ministry of Tourism recognition as a tour operator or travel agent, which brings MDA marketing subsidies.
- Registration on the SEPC-run "Heal in India" portal, for listing and visibility.
- NABH certification for Medical Value Travel facilitators.

The facilitator segment is described as "largely unregulated".

### Cited Findings
- The Ministry of Tourism's 2015 guidelines on wellness and medical tourism extend Marketing Development Assistance (MDA) to JCI- or NABH-accredited hospitals. They also extend it to facilitators who are travel agents or tour operators engaged in medical tourism, for taking part in Ministry-approved fairs, events and road shows (Niche Tourism Division). It is UNCERTAIN whether the 2015 document is still in force. — [MoT Revised Guidelines 20.03.2015](https://tourism.gov.in/sites/default/files/2020-01/REVISED%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20WELLNESS%20&%20MEDICAL%20TOURISM%20AS%20ON%2020.03.2015.pdf); [PIB: Medical Tourism under MDA](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=78315&reg=3&lang=2)
- The MDA guidelines were revised in Nov 2020. The search summaries describe that revision as focused on domestic tourism, and its medical-tourism terms were not located. — [Lok Sabha USQ 2336 (Dec 2023)](https://tourism.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-12/usq.2336%20for%2018.12.2023.pdf); [Rajya Sabha USQ 947 (2021)](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/719712/1/PQ_254_27072021_U947_p347_p348.pdf)
- The MoT publishes "Revised Guidelines for Recognition of Tourism Service Providers", which is voluntary recognition for travel agents and tour operators. — [MoT Facilitation & Standards](https://tourism.gov.in/facilitation-standards-0)
- The "Heal in India" portal is described as the national digital platform built by the Services Export Promotion Council (SEPC) under the Ministry of Commerce. KPMG (July 2025) says medical value travel facilitators "will be encouraged to register" with the government, and once registered get visibility on the medical tourism portal and on Incredible India. KPMG also described the portal as still under development. — [KPMG Heal in India report](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/07/heal-in-india-catalysing-medical-and-wellness-tourism-for-a-healthier-global-future.html); [KPMG PDF](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/07/heal-in-india-catalysing-medical-and-wellness-tourism-for-a-healthier-global-future.pdf)
- The SEPC-linked site has an MVT Facilitator page with a "Register as Overseas Facilitator" option. It claims 1,400+ verified hospitals and 250+ MVT facilitators; these figures are self-reported. — [indiahealthcaretourism.com MVT Facilitators](https://www.indiahealthcaretourism.com/mvt-facilitators); [About Us](https://www.indiahealthcaretourism.com/about-us)
- NABH accredits "Medical Value Travel Facilitators", a segment described as largely unregulated. This is from the KPMG report as summarised by search. — [KPMG PDF](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/07/heal-in-india-catalysing-medical-and-wellness-tourism-for-a-healthier-global-future.pdf)
- Further official background on medical tourism hubs, not read in full: [MoT/PIB "Development of Medical Tourism Hubs" (Dec 2023)](https://tourism.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-12/PIB1987820.pdf); [PIB "Medical and Wellness Tourism in India"](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=158426&ModuleId=3&reg=3&lang=1); [IMA medical tourism page](https://www.ima-india.org/ima/left-side-bar.php?pid=503)

### Inferences
- **No licence is needed to operate, but voluntary credentials are worth getting:**
  - NABH MVTF certification
  - Heal in India registration
  - MoT tour-operator recognition, which is needed for MDA support at fairs
- These credentials are the main trust signals Indian hospitals and the Kazakh embassy are likely to look for. They also help a facilitator obtain hospital "visa invitation letters".
- **NABH or JCI status of partner hospitals is commercially and reputationally important rather than legally required.** It is a condition for hospitals to get MDA support, and the obvious defensible quality filter if a facilitator is later accused of steering patients to poor providers.

### Gaps
- The constitution, mandate and current activity of the **National Medical & Wellness Tourism Board** were not confirmed; no source was retrieved.
- The NABH MVT Facilitator accreditation standard (requirements, fees, validity) was not retrieved.
- No source confirmed whether Heal in India registration is open to foreign-registered facilitators such as a Kazakh LLP, what documents it needs, or whether it is moving towards being mandatory.

## Q3. India: e-Medical and e-Medical Attendant visas, FRRO, and the new immigration reporting duties

### Takeaway
The e-Medical visa allows **three entries**. Commercial sources commonly cite a 60-day stay from first arrival. An official Indian mission advisory says it can be **extended by up to 6 more months, case by case, by the FRRO/FRO**. Up to **two attendants** may get e-Medical Attendant visas linked to the patient. Under the new Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, in force from about Sept 2025, hospitals must record and electronically report foreign patients.

### Cited Findings
- An official Indian mission advisory says a "Triple entry e-Medical Visa may be granted which can be extended by another 6 months on a case-to-case basis on its merit by the Foreigners Regional Registration Officer (FRRO)/ Foreigners Registration Officer (FRO) concerned." — [Consulate General of India, Vladivostok advisory](https://cgivladi.gov.in/page/advisory-for-e-visa-applicants)
- Commercial visa sites describe a triple-entry visa with a 60-day stay. One says it is valid 120 days from issue, with the second and third entries within 60 days of the first. Sources conflict on whether it can be extended. UNCERTAIN; the official FRRO wording above should prevail. — [VisaMind](https://www.visamind.com/en/india/visas/e-medical); [BankBazaar](https://commonfloor.bankbazaar.com/visa/know-about-medical-visa-in-india.html); [indianvisa.it.com](https://www.indianvisa.it.com/indian-visa-extension-options)
- Attendants are capped at two per patient. The patient must already hold an approved e-Medical visa, and attendants generally travel with the patient. One guide says the attendant visa is coterminous with the patient's. — [India-eVisa.org](https://www.india-evisa.org/india-medical-attendant-visa); [DesiNRI attendant guide](https://desinri.com/visa/india-e-medical-attendant-visa-e-medical-attendant-requirements-fees-processing-time-how-to-apply/); [VFS Medical/Medical Attendant checklist](https://services.vfsglobal.com/one-pager/india/united-states-of-america/visa-services/pdf/medical-visa-checklist-updated.pdf)
- Applicants must apply online at least 4 days before arrival, with a passport valid for 6 months and two blank pages. — [Indian e-Visa portal](https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/); [CGI San Francisco e-Visa page](https://www.cgisf.gov.in/page/e-visa/)
- Registration, extension, conversion and exit permits go through the e-FRRO online system. — [indianvisa.it.com](https://www.indianvisa.it.com/indian-visa-extension-options) (commercial source)
- **Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025:**
  - Hospitals and medical institutions must keep records of foreign patients and report them electronically. Births and deaths of foreigners must be reported within 7 days; one source cites Section 10 and Rule 18(7) of the Immigration and Foreigners Order 2025.
  - The Act came into force in Sept 2025.
  - These are secondary explainers only. — [Lawrbit](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/immigration-and-foreigners-act-2025/); [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/new-foreigners-act-2025-key-provisions-exemptions-and-enforcement-reforms/); [Lukmaan IAS (Apr 2026)](https://blog.lukmaanias.com/2026/04/27/immigration-and-foreigners-act-2025/)

### Inferences
- **Plan around the 60-day stay; treat extensions as exceptions.** The facilitator's operating playbook should assume a 60-day stay. Extensions go through e-FRRO on medical merit and need supporting letters from the hospital.
- **Long or repeated treatment needs a different visa.** Courses such as oncology cycles may justify a regular Medical (MED) visa from an Indian mission instead of an e-visa.
- **Patient-tracking data is now regulated in India too.** Hospitals now have statutory duties to report foreign patients, which makes facilitator records (patient identity, attendants, stay addresses) part of a compliance chain.

### Gaps
- The official e-Visa page could not be read, so the following are UNCERTAIN:
  - the exact current stay length
  - whether the e-Medical visa can be obtained "3 times per calendar year"
  - attendant validity
  - fees
- The e-Visa eligibility of Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan and Russia was **not confirmed**. They are believed eligible, but this must be verified.
- The text of the Immigration and Foreigners Act/Order 2025 was not read, including whether *facilitators or accommodation providers* (as distinct from hospitals) have their own reporting duty, such as Form C for hotels and guest houses.

## Q4. India: data protection (DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025)

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified in mid-November 2025 and are being phased in. The Data Protection Board provisions took effect immediately, Consent Manager provisions follow at about 12 months (around Nov 2026), and most operating obligations at about 18 months (around **May 2027**). Until then, the IT Act's 2011 SPDI Rules continue to apply. The exact dates for each tranche differ between sources.

### Cited Findings
- The Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025; some sources say they were gazetted on 14 Nov. Rules 1, 2 and 17 (definitions and the Data Protection Board) took effect immediately. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-); [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/current-affairs/dpdp-rules-2025-notified/?pdf=1)
- About 13 Nov 2026 is cited for Rule 4, with Consent Manager registration opening around then. One search summary also put notice, consent, security, breach and data-principal obligations at that date. That conflicts with other sources putting the bulk of obligations at 18 months, so it is UNCERTAIN. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-); [TCSA roadmap](https://www.tcsa.in/resources/dpdp-rules-2025-implementation-roadmap)
- The final tranche falls at 18 months; sources give 12, 13 or 14 May 2027. — [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1706878/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones); [TCSA](https://www.tcsa.in/resources/dpdp-rules-2025-implementation-roadmap); [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/framework/dpdp)
- The IT Rules 2011 regime stays in force until the phase-in ends. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-)

### Inferences
- **The DPDP Act will likely apply.** An Indian facilitator entity processing Kazakh or Uzbek patients' records in India will likely be a "Data Fiduciary" under the DPDP Act once obligations start, as the Act applies to processing within India. In the meantime, health records are "sensitive personal data" under the 2011 SPDI Rules.
- **Three statutory features need confirming with Indian counsel, as no source was retrieved:**
  - The DPDP Act has no separate "sensitive/health" category; this is my understanding of the Act.
  - Cross-border transfers are allowed except to countries the government blacklists (s.16).
  - The s.17(1)(d) exemption may cover data of non-residents processed under a contract with a foreign person.
- **Data protection laws in several countries apply at once.** The binding constraint is the *source-country* laws (Kazakhstan's localization rule and Uzbekistan's strict localization rule; see Q8 and Q9) as well as the DPDP Act.

### Gaps
- The DPDP Rules' rule-by-rule commencement schedule was not read from the gazette.
- No source was retrieved on DPDP s.16 (cross-border transfer) or s.17(1)(d), or on any MeitY guidance about health data.

## Q5. India: GST and FEMA on facilitation income

### Takeaway
GST changed in 2026. **From 30 Mar 2026 the Finance Act 2026 omitted s.13(8)(b) of the IGST Act.** Before that, place of supply for "intermediary services" was the supplier's location, so an Indian facilitator could not zero-rate commissions from foreign principals. Intermediary services now follow the recipient-location rule and **can qualify as exports** if the export conditions are met. On FEMA, the new FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 apply from 1 Oct 2026. Export proceeds must be realised within **9 months** (12 months if invoiced in INR), and a services EDF is introduced.

### Cited Findings
- Under s.2(13) IGST an intermediary is a broker, agent or facilitator arranging supplies between others, not supplying on its own account. Under the old s.13(8)(b), the place of supply was the supplier's location where either party was outside India. Receiving foreign currency alone therefore did not make the service an export. — [Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/intermediary-services-under-gst-place-of-supply-rules/); [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=117363)
- The Gujarat High Court upheld s.13(8)(b) as constitutionally valid in 2020. — [EY alert (2020)](https://assets.ey.com/content/dam/ey-sites/ey-com/en_in/alerts_pdf/2020/08/hc-upholds-constitutional-validity-of-place-of-supply-provision-relating-to-intermediary-services.pdf)
- In Sept 2025 the GST Council recommended aligning the place of supply for intermediary services with the recipient's location. — [EY (Sept 2025)](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2025/09/gst-council-recommends-export-status-for-intermediary-services)
- Section 157 of the Finance Act 2026 omitted s.13(8)(b) with effect from 30 Mar 2026, the date of Presidential assent. Intermediary services now fall under s.13(2), the recipient's location, and "can qualify as exports subject to prescribed conditions". — [Grant Thornton Bharat](https://www.grantthornton.in/insights/articles/gst-on-intermediary-services/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1037662); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/?p=18253)
- The omission has no saving clause, so the treatment of pre-30 Mar 2026 transactions is contested. — [ICAI CA Journal](https://cajournal.icai.org/article-details/when-the-legislature-erases-a-law-do-omissions-count-as-a-repeal-under-the-general-clauses-act); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1042425)
- Importers of intermediary services may now face more reverse-charge exposure. — [Grant Thornton Bharat](https://www.grantthornton.in/insights/articles/gst-on-intermediary-services/)
- Export of services needs four things: the supplier is in India, the recipient is outside India, the place of supply is outside India, and payment is received in convertible foreign exchange. Acting on one's own account (principal-to-principal) rather than as an intermediary can also support export treatment. — [Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/intermediary-services-under-gst-place-of-supply-rules/)
- **FEMA:**
  - The FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 apply from 1 Oct 2026 and supersede the 2015 export regulations.
  - The realisation period is 9 months generally and 12 months for INR-invoiced or INR-settled exports. For services it runs from the invoice date.
  - A services Export Declaration Form (EDF) is introduced; for non-software services it may be filed on or before the date payment is received.
  - — [TaxGuru guide](https://taxguru.in/?p=1082965); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-notifies-fem-export-and-import-regulations-2026); [EY (Jan 2026)](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2026/01/rbi-issues-export-and-import-regulations-2026.pdf)
- The January 2026 text originally allowed 15 and 18 months; an amendment cut these to 9 and 12. Sources give different dates for the amendment (5 June 2026 vs 22 Sept 2026), so it is UNCERTAIN. — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-restores-export-proceeds-realisation-period-to-9-months/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/rbi-amendments-to-foreign-exchange-management-(export-and-import-of-goods-and-services)-(amendment)-regulations-2026/1850040); [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=88969); [JSA Prism](https://www.jsalaw.com/?p=56623)

### Inferences
The GST treatment depends on the structure. **A chartered accountant must confirm all of these:**
1. **Indian facilitator paid commission by an Indian hospital.** This is a domestic B2B supply, taxable in India.
2. **Indian facilitator charging Kazakh patients directly.** Post-amendment, place of supply follows the recipient, but services that need the recipient to be physically present may follow the place of performance (s.13(3)(b)). Examples are airport pickup, in-hospital interpretation and local logistics. So patient-paid on-the-ground services may be taxable in India even though the patient is foreign. This is an inference, not sourced.
3. **A Kazakh entity receiving commission from an Indian hospital.** After 30 Mar 2026 the place of supply is the Indian recipient, so the hospital may owe reverse-charge IGST on the imported intermediary service. That could make hospitals resist paying commissions offshore.

FEMA: an Indian entity invoicing foreign hospitals or foreign patients must track realisation within 9 months and file the services EDF.

### Gaps
- No CBIC circular specific to medical tourism facilitators was found.
- The applicable GST rate on facilitation or commission services was not retrieved from a primary source.
- Whether the omission of s.13(8)(b) affects the reverse charge on healthcare-adjacent imports was not researched.

## Q6. India: transplant law (THOTA 1994 and THOT Rules 2014) and the scandals that shape scrutiny of facilitators

### Takeaway
Transplants for foreign patients need **prior approval from the hospital or state Authorisation Committee** and a **Form 21 certificate from a senior embassy official** of the patient's country. The certificate confirms the donor–recipient relationship, that the documents are genuine, and that the donation is altruistic. An **Indian donor for a foreign recipient is not permitted unless they are "near relatives".** Recent rackets used forged high-commission documents and agents recruiting at dialysis centres. Facilitators are the obvious point of criminal exposure.

### Cited Findings
- Where a foreign national is involved, prior approval of the Authorisation Committee is required. Rule 20(a) of the 2014 Rules sets the procedure for foreigners. — [Lok Sabha answer AU1351](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2974437/1/AU1351.pdf); [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/organ-donation/acts-related-to-human-organ-donation?lgn=en)
- **Form 21 requirements:**
  - A senior embassy official of the country of origin must certify the donor–recipient relationship on Form 21. If the country has no embassy in India, its government issues the certificate in the same format.
  - Form 21 also certifies that the identification documents are authentic.
  - The Committee and the embassy official must certify that the donation is "out of love and affection", with no financial transaction and no coercion.
  - — [Lok Sabha AU1351](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2974437/1/AU1351.pdf)
- "The transplantation shall not be permitted if the recipient is a foreign national and donor is an Indian national unless they are near relatives." — [Lok Sabha AU1351](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2974437/1/AU1351.pdf)
- Rule 7 sets up the Authorisation Committee. Rule 7(3) requires it to ensure there is no commercial transaction where donor and recipient are not near relatives. The 2014 Rules replaced the 2008 Rules from 27 Mar 2014. Application differed by state: Tamil Nadu applied the 2011 amendment and 2014 Rules only from 2020. — [SCC Online (Madras HC, Oct 2023)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/10/25/madras-high-court-hospitals-cannot-refuse-perform-transplants-from-non-related-organ-donors/)
- **Delhi–Noida case, July 2024:** Delhi Police arrested a doctor, a visiting consultant who had been suspended by her employer Indraprastha Apollo, and associates in a Bangladesh–India kidney ring.
  - About 15 Bangladeshi transplants were performed between 2021 and 2023, mostly at Yatharth Hospital, Noida.
  - Each transplant was charged at ₹25–30 lakh.
  - Forged documents in the name of the Bangladesh High Commission were used to fake donor–recipient relationships.
  - Agents recruited patients at dialysis centres in Bangladesh.
  - — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/international-kidney-transplant-racket-busted-doctor-among-7-held-638426/amp); [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/bangladesh/351496/delhi-doctor-held-for-bangladesh-india-kidney); [Eastern Eye](https://www.easterneye.biz/kidney-trafficking-racket-india)
- A charge sheet in the Delhi case named three Bangladeshis. — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=164289)
- In March 2024 a Gurgaon racket facilitated kidney transplants at Fortis Hospital, Jaipur. — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/823256)
- A Telegraph investigation reported Myanmar citizens being lured to Delhi to sell organs. — [Eastern Eye](https://www.easterneye.biz/kidney-trafficking-racket-india)
- Kazakhstan link: residents of Kazakhstan received scam emails from a purported "Indian clinic" offering $100–300k for kidneys. — [Kazinform press review](https://www.inform.kz/ru/indiyskaya-klinika-hochet-kupit-pochki-kazahstancev-obzor-respublikanskoy-pressy-za-31-yanvarya_a2626462)
- Other reforms, not read in detail, include NOTTO IDs for transplants. — [Khan Global Studies](https://currentaffairs.khanglobalstudies.com/notto-id-for-organ-transplants/); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/reforming-organ-transplantation)

### Inferences
- **Transplant cases need tight internal controls.** The facilitator should handle related-donor living transplants only, collect documents only through the Kazakh, Uzbek or Kyrgyz embassy's Form 21 process, and never be involved in finding a donor or paying for one.
- **Agents are the weak point regulators target.** Since the 2024 rackets turned on forged embassy papers and recruiting agents, police and hospitals may scrutinise any facilitator handling transplant patients. Documenting the chain of custody of embassy certificates is a defensive necessity.

### Gaps
- The text of Rule 20 and Form 21 was not read.
- Post-2024 Ministry of Health and NOTTO advisories on foreigner transplants were not retrieved; they may include mandatory NOTTO registration or a cap on the share of foreign patients.
- The Kazakh embassy's practice for issuing Form 21 certificates was not researched.

## Q7. Kazakhstan: advertising, licensing, doctor ethics, personal data, and company or tax set-up

### Takeaway
The biggest Kazakh risk is **advertising**. The Ministry of Health says advertising of medical services (care) is governed by **Article 56 of the Health Code (No. 360-VI of 7 Jul 2020)** and **Order ҚР ДСМ-105/2020** (21 Sep 2020). It also says such advertising is allowed **only if the advertiser holds a state licence for the advertised type of medical activity**.
- An Indian hospital has no Kazakh licence, so openly advertising specific Indian treatments in Kazakhstan is legally exposed.
- Whether pure facilitation (logistics) needs a medical licence is **not established**.
- Kazakhstan's data law requires personal data to be **stored in Kazakhstan** (Law 94-V, Art. 12), with cross-border transfer under Art. 16. A May 2026 draft decree to relax this is pending.

### Cited Findings
**Advertising**
- The Ministry of Health (Almaty oblast department of the Committee for Medical and Pharmaceutical Control) says advertising of medical services is governed by Art. 56 of the Code "On public health and the healthcare system" and Order ҚР ДСМ-105/2020 of 21 Sep 2020, "Rules for advertising medical services (care)". It adds: "реклама медицинских услуг (помощи) осуществляется при наличии государственной лицензии на рекламируемый вид медицинской деятельности" ("advertising of medical services (care) may be carried out only with a state licence for the advertised type of medical activity"). — [gov.kz, DKMFK Almaty oblast](https://www.gov.kz/memleket/entities/dkmfk-almaty-oblast/press/news/details/1268034?lang=ru)
- The current Code's advertising article requires advertising of services to be reliable and recognisable without special knowledge, and to avoid comparisons with other services. — [Adilet: Code K2000000360 (English)](https://adilet.zan.kz/eng/docs/K2000000360)
- The old 2009 Code required medical-services advertising to be truthful about cost and expected results. It also banned advertising services by persons without a medical licence. — [WIPO Lex (2009 Code)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/496178); [prg.kz old version](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/31649625/4/190000)
- The general Law on Advertising says advertising of medical services is regulated by healthcare legislation. — [Law on Advertising (IBFAN copy)](https://www.ibfan.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Kazakhstan-2015-Code-Regulations.pdf)
- Separate rules on advertising medicines and medical devices need a pre-approval from the expert centre and must be in Kazakh and Russian. — [Adilet V2000021872](https://adilet.zan.kz/eng/docs/V2000021872); [Lexology Q&A (Oct 2024)](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0344019c-12f2-4d98-bfb1-3fa4ad7ef903); [MedStandard](https://en.medstandard.kz/services/evaluation-of-advertising-materials-for-advertising-medicinal-products-and-medical-devices-kazakhstan/)

**Licensing and the state route to treatment abroad**
- Medical-activity licences are issued by territorial departments of the Committee for Medical and Pharmaceutical Control of the Ministry of Health. — [eGov.kz](https://egov.kz/cms/ru/articles/bus_pharm/medical-licensing)
- A state-funded route to treatment abroad exists. It needs a consilium of at least three leading specialists, and the "working body" may issue a guarantee letter of payment to the foreign clinic. — [Adilet V2100022866](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2100022866); [eGov service](https://egov.kz/cms/ru/services/health_care/pass-132-5_mz)

**Doctor ethics**
- The **Code of Honor for medical and pharmaceutical workers** was adopted under Art. 274(2) of the 2020 Health Code. Its principles include impartiality, honesty and objectivity, and independence. — [Adilet V2000021890](https://adilet.zan.kz/eng/docs/V2000021890)
- The 2020 health reform rules out sponsor support, bonuses and conference expenses paid to medical workers by pharmaceutical companies. Promotion by pharma representatives to doctors during working hours is restricted. — [PharmaBoardroom](https://pharmaboardroom.com/articles/kazakhstans-code-to-reform-their-healthcare-system/); [Conventus Law / Mondaq](https://conventuslaw.com/report/kazakhstan-promotion-of-medicines-are-you-ready-to/)

**Personal data**
- Law 94-V of 21 May 2013, Art. 12, requires storage "on the territory of the Republic", and this has been reinforced by ministry rules, including a 2025 order. Cross-border transfer is governed by Art. 16. — [AmCham Kazakhstan issue brief (15 May 2026)](https://amcham.kz/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-05-15_AmCham_DataLocalization_Issue-Brief_ENG_v5.pdf); [Uppersetup](https://uppersetup.com/en/blog/personal-data-and-localisation-in-kazakhstan-in-2026-law-no)
- Commercial summaries list the Art. 16 grounds as consent, adequacy, contracts and authorised-body approval. These need verifying. — [Clym](https://regulations.clym.io/law-no-94-v-kazakhstan); [Kiteworks brief](https://www.kiteworks.com/sites/default/files/resources/kiteworks-brief-enables-kazakhstan-personal-data-law-compliance.pdf)
- The Digital Code (Law No. 255-VIII), signed 9 Jan 2026, widened the definition of personal data. One analysis says it left localization and cross-border duties in Law 94-V; its effective date is cited as 12 Jul 2026, which needs confirming. — [AmCham brief](https://amcham.kz/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-05-15_AmCham_DataLocalization_Issue-Brief_ENG_v5.pdf); [Uppersetup](https://uppersetup.com/en/blog/personal-data-and-localisation-in-kazakhstan-in-2026-law-no)
- A draft decree (May 2026) would remove mandatory domestic storage for most categories of personal data and allow cross-border transfers. It was **not confirmed as adopted**. — [AmCham brief](https://amcham.kz/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-05-15_AmCham_DataLocalization_Issue-Brief_ENG_v5.pdf)

**Company and tax set-up**
- Astana Hub benefits now sit in the new Tax Code (No. 214-VIII of 18 Jul 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026). Participants must meet a "90/10" income test under Art. 17: at least 90% of income from priority IT activities. Breaching it costs the benefits and the status. New participation rules came in by Order 703/НҚ (31 Dec 2025, effective 23 Jan 2026), amended July 2026. — [Astana Hub blog](https://astanahub.com/en/blog/pravilo-90-10-v-astana-hub-znachenie-struktury-dokhodov-dlia-primeneniia-nalogovykh-lgot); [Uppersetup](https://uppersetup.com/en/blog/astana-hub-in-2026-new-participation-rules-the-auditor); [Revera Law](https://kazakhstan.revera.legal/en/info-centr/reviews/42-gajd-po-trebovaniyam-astana-hub-i-nalogovym-lgotam/)
- The eligible activities are IT categories such as software, AI, fintech and edtech. The source is promotional. — [about-kazakhstan.com](https://about-kazakhstan.com/astana-hub-guide/)

### Inferences
- **Advertising is the critical Kazakh risk.** If the licence condition applies to anyone advertising medical services, then a facilitator running ads for treatments ("knee replacement in Delhi for $X") is advertising medical services without a Kazakh licence, and so is the Indian hospital being promoted. A lower-risk approach is to market the *facilitation service*: travel, visas, interpretation, second-opinion logistics. That means avoiding treatment claims, prices for medical procedures, outcome promises and comparisons. Kazakh counsel must confirm this reading of Art. 56 and Order ДСМ-105/2020.
- **Clinical opinions in Kazakhstan likely need a medical licence.** If the doctor-founder gives clinical opinions to patients in Kazakhstan, such as reviewing scans or recommending a procedure, that is likely "medical activity", which needs a licensed medical organisation or appropriate certification. This is unconfirmed. Splitting the business into a licensed medical consultation entity and a separate travel/facilitation entity is a common pattern.
- **Data flows must start in Kazakhstan.** Collect and store patient files in Kazakhstan first. Send them to Indian hospitals only with explicit written consent (Art. 16), and use a Kazakh-hosted database for the master copy.
- **Astana Hub is unlikely to fit.** A medical tourism facilitator will probably fail the 90/10 IT-income test, unless it is structured as a software platform company earning mainly platform or SaaS income. That is doubtful.
- **The same ethics logic applies in Kazakhstan.** For a Kazakh-registered doctor, payments from foreign hospitals tied to referrals would conflict with the "impartiality / independence" principles of the Code of Honor.

### Gaps
- The full text of Order ҚР ДСМ-105/2020 and the current Art. 56 was not read. It is not established whether the licence condition binds a *non-medical* advertiser, or advertising for foreign clinics.
- No source states whether "organising treatment abroad" or medical-tourism facilitation is a licensed activity or sub-type under Kazakhstan's licensing law.
- No Kazakh source was found that specifically bans doctors from receiving referral fees; the conflict-of-interest provisions of the Health Code were not located. Medical-secrecy rules for health data were not researched.
- No source was retrieved on IP (individual entrepreneur) versus LLP (TOO) tax regimes under the 2026 Tax Code.

## Q8. Uzbekistan: advertising medical services and personal-data localization

### Takeaway
Uzbekistan is the strictest market on both counts.
- **Data:** Article 27-1 of the Personal Data Law (in force 15 Apr 2021) requires citizens' personal data to be stored on technical means *located in Uzbekistan* and registered in the State Register. This has kept out Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal.
- **Advertising:** The Competition Committee says **medical services not listed in a medical licence may not be advertised**, and bans outcome guarantees, with fines under the Advertising Law.

### Cited Findings
- **Localization:**
  - The 2021 amendment added the duty to store citizens' data on technical means in Uzbekistan registered in the State Register of personal data databases, effective 15 Apr 2021. — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/data-protection/1197754/the-law-on-personal-data-has-been-amended)
  - Apple and Google declined to integrate their payment systems because of the law, and the Central Bank says a review is underway. — [Kun.uz (Dec 2024)](https://kun.uz/en/news/2024/12/26/uzbekistans-data-localization-law-hinders-entry-of-apple-pay-and-google-pay)
  - Uzbekistan is the only Central Asian country without PayPal. — [Intellinews](https://www.intellinews.com/why-does-uzbekistan-remain-sole-country-in-central-asia-where-paypal-is-not-operational-390961)
  - A July 2025 report estimated losses of up to $45bn from the localization law. — [Daryo](https://daryo.uz/en/2025/07/16/uzbekistan-loses-up-to-45bn-annually-due-to-data-localization-law-report-finds/)
  - At a July 2025 meeting with Google, Visa and Mastercard, experts said the provision is vague, including about who counts as the "operator". — [Kun.uz (Jul 2025)](https://ad.kun.uz/en/news/2025/07/22/experts-urge-uzbekistan-to-revise-data-localization-rules-to-support-innovation)
  - EUI digital trade database entry: [EUI DTI](https://dti.eui.eu/database/page/166)
- **Advertising:**
  - The Competition Committee says medical services not specified in a medical practice licence may not be advertised.
  - Guaranteeing outcomes, promising absolute safety, or denying contraindications may breach the law.
  - Improper advertising falls under Art. 16 of the Law "On Advertising", with a fine of 70 times the base calculation value under Art. 47.
  - — [UzDaily](https://www.uzdaily.uz/en/uzbekistan-competition-committee-urges-compliance-with-medical-advertising-rules/); [Competition Committee (raqobat.gov.uz)](https://raqobat.gov.uz/en/?p=71908)
- The 2022 Advertising Law bans celebrities, medical staff, or people who look like doctors in medicine adverts. — [Kun.uz (Mar 2022)](https://kun.uz/en/news/2022/03/17/celebrities-and-doctors-banned-from-advertising-medicines-in-uzbekistan); [GRATA International](https://gratanet.com/publications/advertising-of-medicines-and-dietary-supplements-in-the-republic-of-uzbekistan); [Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/uzbekistan-new-advertising-law-seeks-to-tighten-standards)

### Inferences
- **Collected Uzbek data must be held in Uzbekistan.** A facilitator collecting Uzbek patients' data through its own website, CRM or WhatsApp intake is likely within Art. 27-1 and would need the data held on Uzbek-located servers registered with the State Register. Using a licensed Uzbek partner (a local tour operator or clinic) to collect the data and forward it with consent is the practical workaround. Uzbek counsel must confirm.
- **Avoid a doctor-led advertising style.** The founder's appearance as a doctor in adverts is restricted for medicines. For services, the "licence-scope" rule makes advertising foreign treatments risky, as in Kazakhstan.

### Gaps
- The search budget ran out before Uzbekistan could be checked for:
  - any amendment to Art. 27-1 in 2025–26 (the Central Bank review)
  - penalty amounts and actions against foreign websites
  - whether medical-tourism agencies need a licence
- The lex.uz texts were not read.

## Q10. Payments: Kazakh, Uzbek and Russian patients paying Indian hospitals; cash import into India

### Takeaway
No source was found that describes Kazakh or Uzbek payment channels to Indian hospitals. Kazakhstan's currency rules generally require the bank to see a contract or invoice above thresholds. ### Inferences
- **Facilitators are exposed in two ways:**
  1. As a "service provider" under consumer law in both countries, for misrepresentation, mismatched fees, or failures in logistics and disclosure. This is distinct from medical negligence, which sits with the hospital and doctor.
  2. The doctor-founder faces extra exposure: if they gave the clinical recommendation, a patient may frame a claim as medical negligence against the doctor personally, in either country.
- **Contractual mitigants:**
  - a written agreement making clear that the facilitator does not provide treatment
  - written disclosure of any hospital payments
  - itemised, non-refundable vs refundable fees
  - hospital estimates passed on unaltered
  - no outcome promises, which is also required by the Kazakh and Uzbek advertising rules
- **Insurance to explore:** professional indemnity / errors-and-omissions cover for the facilitator entity; the doctor's own medical malpractice cover in each country of registration; and international health and medical-travel complication insurance for patients. None were researched.

### Gaps
- No case law was found on facilitator liability in India or Kazakhstan.
- The availability and cost of professional indemnity or E&O cover for medical tourism facilitators in India and Kazakhstan was not researched.
- Kazakhstan's Consumer Protection Law and its application to agents of foreign services was not researched.

## Q12. Summary risk map: what is allowed, what is forbidden, and which licences or approvals are needed (synthesis of Q1–Q11)

### Takeaway
The model that best survives regulation is a **non-doctor-controlled facilitation company** that:
- earns **transparent, patient-paid service fees** (or disclosed, fixed fees for services hospitals actually receive);
- **markets logistics, not treatments**, in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan;
- **keeps patient data local** first (Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan) and transfers it with written consent;
- leaves **transplant cases** to formal embassy and Authorisation Committee channels.

The riskiest model is per-patient hospital commissions to a company owned by the dual-registered doctor, advertised with treatment prices and outcome claims.
