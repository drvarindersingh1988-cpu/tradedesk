# Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan): outbound medical tourism market, Indian hospital presence, local agencies, competing destinations

Partway through, the shared web-search budget ran out. So almost every finding below comes from **search-result snippets or summaries, not full-page reads**. ## 1. Market size: patients going abroad, spend, destination split, treatment mix, India's inbound numbers

### Takeaway
Kazakhstan is the only country in the region with a published full-market estimate. A 2023 study (Ulagat Consulting Group with the journal "Ұлағатты Медицина") counted just under **114,000 outbound medical tourists spending $948.1M**. Industry associations give much lower estimates of 40–60k a year. Korea and Turkey are the best-documented destinations, at roughly 14k and 12–15k Kazakhs a year. India is estimated at up to 6k Kazakhs a year, but that figure is unsourced.

Uzbekistan's border statistics record roughly **4k outbound "treatment" trips a month in early 2025**, about 45–50k a year if annualised. No reliable outbound figures exist for Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan or Turkmenistan. Tajiks mostly go to Uzbekistan, with 59k patients there in 2025.

India's medical visas fell 22% in 2024 because of Bangladesh. Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan were among the few sources that grew. I found no official country-level Indian figure for any Central Asian state.

### Cited Findings
**Kazakhstan: total market**
- In the first public study of Kazakhstan's outbound medical tourism, Ulagat Consulting Group and the journal "Ұлағатты Медицина" estimated 2023 outbound medical tourists at "just under 114 thousand", with total spending of **$948.1 million** — [bes.media](https://bes.media/news/na-lechenie-za-rubezhom-kazahstantsi-potratili-948-mln-dollarov-v-2023-godu-issledovanie-9d568c/) (also the [AMP version](https://bes.media/amp/na-lechenie-za-rubezhom-kazahstantsi-potratili-948-mln-dollarov-v-2023-godu-issledovanie-9d568c/)). I could not open the full study or any country-by-country spending split.
- The study groups destinations into three types:
  - Western countries with advanced medicine: **Germany, Israel**.
  - Countries actively recruiting medical tourists: **South Korea, Turkey, India**.
  - Neighbouring countries: **Russia, Uzbekistan**.
  — [bes.media](https://bes.media/news/na-lechenie-za-rubezhom-kazahstantsi-potratili-948-mln-dollarov-v-2023-godu-issledovanie-9d568c/)

### Inferences
- **Kazakhstan spend per patient:** the Ulagat estimate works out to about **$8,300 per medical tourist** ($948.1M ÷ ~114k). That fits a mix of high-cost cancer care in Germany and Israel with cheaper check-ups in Korea and Turkey.
- **Korea check-up spend:** KRW 6.08M is roughly **$4,300–4,500** at 2024 exchange rates (my conversion). That suggests the Korean flow is dominated by check-ups and dermatology rather than major surgery.
- **Two Kazakh market sizes can be reconciled.** The ~114k (Ulagat) and 40–60k (association) figures likely differ in definition, e.g. counting companions and diagnostics-only trips. A defensible planning range is **50–115k Kazakh medical trips a year, about $0.5–1.0bn**.
- **India's share of Kazakhstan.** On the available (unofficial) number, India gets ≤6k Kazakhs a year, i.e. **~5–10%** of the market. It sits well behind Korea (~14k) and Turkey (~12–15k).
- **Uzbekistan outbound volume.** Border data run at ~3.5–4k treatment trips a month in early 2025, implying **~45–50k a year**. This is likely an undercount, because many patients declare "tourism" or "visiting relatives". India is widely described as a key destination for complex surgery, especially liver and kidney cases (see section 2, Aakash). Uzbekistan's rise in Indian medical visas in 2024, when the overall total fell 22%, supports this.
- **The intra-regional hubs compete directly with India for lower-acuity cases.** Uzbekistan (~86k inbound in 2025) and Kazakhstan (~80k inbound in 2024) both do this, especially for Tajik and Kyrgyz patients who cannot afford long-haul travel.

### Gaps
- **Ulagat study:** I could not obtain the full study, its methodology, or its per-country patient and spending split. I found no newer (2024/2025) total for Kazakhstan.
- **Uzbekistan:** no full-year 2024 or 2025 outbound "treatment" total, and no Uzbek-government estimate of spending abroad.
- **Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan:** no reliable outbound totals or spending figures.
- **India by source country:** no official medical / e-medical visa counts for any of the five countries for 2022–2026. That needs MHA/MEA or Lok Sabha country-wise annexes, or the India Tourism Statistics "FTAs by country and purpose" table. I could not reach these PDFs.
- **Treatment-category split:** no reliable breakdown (oncology, cardiac, ortho, transplant, IVF, neuro) by destination, beyond qualitative notes: oncology and diagnosis for Kazakhs; check-ups and dermatology for Korea; transplants for Kyrgyz going to India and Turkey; liver and kidney for Uzbeks going to India.

## 2. Indian hospitals' presence in Central Asia and how they recruit

### Takeaway
India's physical presence is concentrated in **Tashkent**:
- **Info / representative offices:** Apollo (run by UMT/Medicon), Max, Fortis (Tashkent plus Andijan) and Medanta (via a rep office and a partnership with Vitamed Medical).
- **An owned hospital:** Aakash Healthcare's 50–60-bed Aakash Multispecialty Hospital (since Nov 2022).

**Almaty** has an "Apollo Hospitals" medical-tourism centre. I found no Indian hospital office in Astana, Bishkek, Dushanbe or Ashgabat. Recruitment relies on local licensees/partners, free consultation days with visiting Indian doctors (often hosted in local clinics), and follow-up consultations for returning patients.

### Inferences
- **Uzbekistan is where Indian hospitals have invested on the ground; Kazakhstan has little visible Indian hospital presence.**
  - Uzbekistan has multiple Big-5 info offices, Andijan and Namangan regional points, and one owned hospital.
  - Kazakhstan has one Apollo-branded centre in Almaty and nothing visible in Astana.
  - This matches the Kazakh destination ranking, where Korea and Turkey lead and India is ≤6k a year.
  - Kazakhstan is therefore the bigger white space for an India-focused venture, but also the harder sell.
- The Apollo office is run by a local operator (UMT/Medicon), and Medanta works through Vitamed. This suggests the common model is a **local licensee or partner clinic that hosts "information centres"**, rather than wholly owned offices. A new venture could compete for these partnerships or aggregate across hospitals.
- Visiting-doctor camps in local clinics (Tashkent Medical Academy, ProfMedService Shymkent) appear to be the main lead-generation tool, often branded as "humanitarian" or "free consultation".

### Gaps
- I could not verify any presence of Artemis, BLK-Max, Narayana, Kokilaben, Manipal or Aster in Central Asia. I also found no Indian hospital office in Astana, Bishkek, Dushanbe or Ashgabat.
- I found no data on patient volumes generated by each office, referral commissions paid to local doctors, or how interpreters are staffed.
- The Medanta Tashkent office status is unresolved (conflicting addresses; one listing "temporarily closed"). I found no 2024–2026 update on Aakash Tashkent.

## 3. Local agencies in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan selling treatment abroad

## 4. How competing destinations recruit Central Asian patients

### Takeaway
**South Korea** is the most institutionally organised competitor:
- A **KHIDI regional office in Almaty** covering all of the CIS.
- City-level campaigns: Busan and Incheon roadshows in Almaty, timed with the new **Busan–Almaty direct flight**, plus a roadshow in Bishkek.
- Strong pull in check-ups and dermatology, with Kazakh patients the **highest spenders per head** in Korea.
- Korean hospitals are also building inside Uzbekistan, which recognises Korean medical licences.

**Turkey** relies on hospital-group representative offices (Acıbadem in Tashkent since 2014 and in Almaty), a large agency ecosystem, and USHAŞ naming Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan as target markets.

**Germany and Israel** are premium oncology and diagnostic destinations, with Germany seen as expensive. **Russia's EMC** is expanding into the CIS. I found no specific information on UAE recruitment.

## 5. Government-funded treatment abroad (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan) and whether a facilitator can work with it

### Takeaway
Kazakhstan's state programme is small. In 2025 (to 1 December) it covered 77 patients: **70 treated in foreign clinics** and 7 treated at home by visiting foreign specialists, at a cost of **over 1bn tenge**.

A Ministry of Health commission decides on each case under the 2021 rules (order ҚР ДСМ-45). The Social Health Insurance Fund (ФСМС) prepares the paperwork and sends patients to the clinics. Children and transplant cases (e.g. bone-marrow transplants) dominate. India is a recurring destination alongside Turkey, Germany, Russia and Belarus. I could **not** verify the "2025: Germany 24, Turkey 17, India 13" split. I found nothing on an Uzbek equivalent.

### Cited Findings
- **2025 volume:** as of 1 Dec 2025, **77 people** received budget-funded high-tech care. **70** were treated in foreign clinics and 7 in Kazakh hospitals during master-classes with foreign specialists. Total programme spending was **>1bn tenge**. Trips patients arrange themselves are not reimbursed — [Turan Times](https://turantimes.kz/obschestvo/65658-kazahstancev-mogut-napravit-na-lechenie-za-rubezh-kto-mozhet-pretendovat-na-pomosch.html); [inbusiness.kz](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/last/70-kazahstancev-proshli-lechenie-v-zarubezhnyh-klinikah-za-schet-gosudarstva)
- In another report, more than 70 patients were sent abroad since the start of the year, **19 of them children for bone-marrow transplantation**. The year was unclear but probably 2025 — [inbusiness.kz](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/last/bolee-70-kazahstancev-poluchili-lechenie-za-rubezhom-za-sch)
- **2024 (MoH data, as reported in Kazakh media):** 80 people were treated in clinics in Turkey, Germany, Russia, Belarus and India, 67 of them children. About 100 Kazakhstanis a year are sent. I could not pin the exact outlet; it appeared alongside [cmn.kz](https://cmn.kz/lechenie-grazhdan-za-rubezhom/) and [inbusiness.kz](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/last/bolee-70-kazahstancev-poluchili-lechenie-za-rubezhom-za-sch) in the results
- **Cost per case:** about **13M tenge on average**, and up to 35M tenge for complex operations. Funding comes from the guaranteed free medical care package (ГОБМП). Same attribution caveat as the 2024 item — [cmn.kz](https://cmn.kz/lechenie-grazhdan-za-rubezhom/)
- **2022:** **71 children** were sent abroad for more than 1bn tenge. The main countries were **Turkey, Germany, India, Russia** **[pre-2024]** — [Kazpravda](https://kazpravda.kz/n/v-kakie-strany-otpravlyayut-kazahstanskih-detey-lechitsya-za-schet-byudzheta/)
- **Range of countries:** clinics in Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Turkey, South Korea, India, Germany, Israel and Japan have received patients. Destinations in 2019–2023 included Turkey, Russia, India, Belarus, Germany, South Korea, Spain and Ukraine **[pre-2024]** — [Kazpravda](https://kazpravda.kz/n/v-kakie-strany-otpravlyayut-kazahstanskih-detey-lechitsya-za-schet-byudzheta/); [inbusiness.kz](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/last/70-kazahstancev-proshli-lechenie-v-zarubezhnyh-klinikah-za-schet-gosudarstva)
- China is excluded from the list — [Astana TV](https://astanatv.kz/ru/news/8099/)
- **Legal basis:**
  - Current rules: MoH order of 26 May 2021, № ҚР ДСМ-45, "Rules for sending citizens for treatment abroad at budget expense".
  - Predecessor rules (2015): [Adilet V1500011795](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V1500011795)
  - Process: a MoH commission decides, the Social Health Insurance Fund (ФСМС) is the working body, and eligibility requires that the technology is not available in Kazakhstan.
  — [Adilet](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V1500011795); [egov.kz quota guide](https://egov.kz/cms/ru/articles/health_care/2Fkvota); [PharmNews.kz](https://pharmnews.kz/news/kvota-na-besplatnoe-lechenie-za-rubezhom-komu-polagaetsya-i-kak-ee-poluchit_21991)
- **Priority categories (sources conflict):** one source lists children, pregnant women and transplant patients with a related donor as priority. The Social Health Insurance Fund has said there are **no** priority categories — [Turan Times](https://turantimes.kz/obschestvo/65658-kazahstancev-mogut-napravit-na-lechenie-za-rubezh-kto-mozhet-pretendovat-na-pomosch.html); [nur.kz](https://www.nur.kz/health/medical-conditions/1901727-kakie-zabolevaniya-kazahstantsev-lechatsya-za-rubezhom-za-schet-gosudarstva/)

### Inferences
- **The state channel is worth roughly $1.5–2.5M a year** (70–100 cases × ~13M tenge ≈ $25k each; my conversion at ~500–520 KZT/USD). It is too small to anchor a business on its own. It is still valuable for **credibility**: being an approved receiving hospital for paediatric bone-marrow transplants or complex paediatric surgery signals quality to the much larger self-pay market.
- India already receives state-funded Kazakh children in every year I could see (2022, 2024). A facilitator's realistic role is to help Indian hospitals get onto the Social Health Insurance Fund's list of approved foreign clinics. It could also provide in-country case coordination and Russian/Kazakh-language support. Selling to patients directly within this channel is not realistic, because the state picks the clinic.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the "2025: Germany 24, Turkey 17, India 13" country split. No search result reproduced it, so it needs the original Social Health Insurance Fund (fms.kz) or MoH release.
- I could not determine how foreign clinics are contracted (direct contract with the Social Health Insurance Fund versus tender, payment terms, whether intermediaries are allowed).
- I found nothing on Uzbekistan's state-funded treatment abroad, its procurement, or Kyrgyz, Tajik or Turkmen equivalents. My searches ran out before I could cover this.

## 6. Direct flights, travel times, visas and language support

### Takeaway
- **Tashkent:** the best-connected city to India, with nonstop Delhi flights by Uzbekistan Airways, IndiGo, Centrum Air and Taban Air, about 13 a week as of Sept 2026.
