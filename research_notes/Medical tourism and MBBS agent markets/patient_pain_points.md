# Pain points and unmet needs of Russian-speaking and Central Asian medical tourists (and their relatives) in India, with Turkey and Korea for comparison

Method note for the report writer: all evidence below comes from web-search result excerpts gathered in October 2026. Full-page fetching failed for every domain in this session (DNS errors), and the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Quotes are therefore taken from search-result excerpts of the named pages, not from a full reading of each article. Where an excerpt could be misattributed, or where sources disagree, this is flagged. Material older than 2022 is marked **[OLDER]**.

## 1. Before travel: choosing a hospital or doctor, trust, quotes, price transparency, agents and scams

### Takeaway
The most consistent pre-travel complaint is a price set low enough to win the patient that then rises after arrival. It comes with an intermediary who becomes unreachable or demands money upfront, and with "package" quotes whose exclusions are unclear. Independent reviews are scarce. Most Russian-language "reviews" of Indian hospitals sit on facilitator-owned aggregator sites, are few in number and cannot be verified. Commission-driven steering is built into the business model in both India (20–35% claimed) and Korea (30–70% reported).

### Cited Findings
- **Low quote, then higher price after arrival, then the intermediary disappears (Uzbekistan, 2023).** In an Oct 2023 Kun.uz interview, Dr. Vivek Vij, head of transplant at Fortis Hospital, said many Uzbek patients who went to India had been deceived. In the pattern he described, the price rises after the patient arrives or upfront payment is demanded, and the go-between becomes unreachable. He called confusion over money "the most frequent problem": patients are drawn in by a price far below other hospitals. He said each treatment is sold as a "package" with a mix of included and excluded services, and that patients should clarify what is covered before travelling. — [Kun.uz, 25 Oct 2023](https://kun.uz/news/2023/10/25/hind-shifokori-hindistonda-davolanishni-istagan-bemorlarga-tavsiyalar-berdi) (attribution comes from a search-result summary. A second search did not return the same article, so check the text before quoting the doctor by name.)
- **Hospital prices about 5x what intermediaries promised (Tashkent patient who died, 2019) [OLDER].** The son of a Tashkent woman who died during cancer treatment in India said intermediaries (an Indian citizen and his Uzbek wife) deceived the family. He said the clinic priced medicines about five times higher than the intermediaries had promised. The family had expected roughly $1,000, and he said the clinic was asking $1,500–5,000 per day. Short of money, he asked for her to be flown home. The intermediaries gave a different version, which the article also reports. — [EADaily, 12 Jun 2019](https://eadaily.com/ru/news/2019/06/12/bolshe-nichego-ne-bolit-grazhdanka-uzbekistana-umerla-na-lechenii-v-indii)
- **Paid for a "professor" and saw a junior doctor; coordination and interpreter "of very doubtful quality."** Review quotes on a Russian aggregator: "Всё, что было оговорено в контракте, выполнено, но услуги очень сомнительного качества, начиная от помощи и координации в клинике и оканчивая переводчиком" (everything in the contract was delivered, but the services were of very doubtful quality, from help and coordination in the clinic to the interpreter). Also: "За те деньги, которые я заплатил, хотелось получить консультацию профессора, а меня принял простой врач. Да и тот ничего нового не сказал" (for the money I paid I wanted a professor's consultation, but an ordinary doctor saw me, and he told me nothing new). The reviews are undated. — [Hospitals-travel.ru, India page](https://hospitals-travel.ru/countries/india)
- **The cost burden falls on families, who turn to crowdfunding and loans (Kazakhstan).** A Kazakh family whose relative was treated for cancer said there were almost no problems with the quality of the Indian doctors: "Самые большие трудности у нас были с финансами" (our biggest difficulties were financial). Treatment in India had already cost 20 million tenge, paid for with loans and borrowing. Another family chose a Turkish clinic because it offered a free consultation with a professor, through an interpreter. — [Bes.media, "Бесплатно, но поздно…"](https://bes.media/news/besplatno-no-pozdno-pochemu-kazahstantsi-sobirayut-dengi-na-lechenie-raka/) (publication date not confirmed)
- **Facilitator commissions in India.** A hospital-marketing consultancy says most Indian hospitals acquire international patients through facilitators who take a 20–35% commission on the total treatment value. This is a vendor source and the figure is not independently corroborated. — [Ichelon Consulting, 2026](https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/medical-tourism-marketing-india-hospitals-2026)
- **Commission economics in Korea, for comparison.** A Korean ministry official warned that brokers demand 30–70% of medical fees from hospitals as commission. Struggling or newly opened clinics accept these terms, "and the quality of the treatment is not guaranteed." — [THE AsiaN, "Medical tourism faces credibility crisis"](https://theasian.asia/archives/46325). A commercial guide estimates that unregistered brokers in Korea outnumber the roughly 1,800 registered agencies by 3–5 times and add 15–30% to bills. Korea's Medical Act Art. 27-3 requires registration for anyone who refers foreign patients for a fee. — [Beautrek (commercial, unverified)](https://beautrek.com/articles/unlicensed-agents). Another agency says patients often believe they are hiring a translator or guide while the agent is in fact earning a commission on the surgery. — [MySeoulSecret (commercial agency, conflict of interest)](https://myseoulsecret.com/korean-medical-tourism-agency)
- **Reviews are thin, mostly hosted by facilitators, and cannot be verified.** Aggregator counts found: Artemis, 17 reviews on Bookimed; Apollo Indraprastha, 9 reviews on Bookimed; Fortis Gurgaon, 13 reviews and "100% recommend" on ClinicsOnCall; Medanta, rated "4.1 from 5 reviews" on one aggregator. — [Bookimed Artemis](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/clinic/artemis-health-institute/), [Bookimed Apollo](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/clinic/apollo-hospitals/), [ClinicsOnCall Fortis](https://clinicsoncall.com/clinic/klinika-fortis-v-guargone/), [Oncoland Medanta](https://oncoland.ru/india/clinic/institut-medicinu-medanta). ClinicsOnCall also hosts one sharp warning against the orthopaedics department of a well-known clinic, which cannot be verified. — [ClinicsOnCall India doctors](https://clinicsoncall.com/en/doctors/country-india/)
- **Ads overstate doctors' capabilities, according to an academic study of patient narratives [OLDER, 2020].** In a qualitative study of online narratives from health travellers to India, complaints about provider interaction were tied to "inaccurate web-based advertisements of the doctor's capabilities and specialties" and to general poor communication. — [JMIR 2020, "Internet Narratives Focused on Health Travelers' Experiences in India"](https://doi.org/10.2196/15665)
- **Second opinion is used as a sales hook rather than given as independent advice.** Families pick destinations according to who offers a free professor consultation (the Turkey example above). — [Bes.media](https://bes.media/news/besplatno-no-pozdno-pochemu-kazahstantsi-sobirayut-dengi-na-lechenie-raka/). Some intermediaries advertise "без посредников" (no intermediaries) while themselves being lead-generation platforms. — [Hospitals-travel.ru](https://hospitals-travel.ru/countries/india)
- **Prepayment scams (Turkey, 2026).** A Turkish doctor quoted by RIA Novosti (1 Jul 2026) described a scheme aimed at Russians. A check-up is offered cheaply, and after the prepayment "организаторы перестают выходить на связь или выясняется, что никакой записи в клинику не существует" (the organisers stop answering, or it turns out no clinic appointment ever existed). She advised paying only through official clinic sites or licensed agencies and "не переводить деньги частным лицам без документального подтверждения" (not transferring money to private individuals without documentary proof). — [RIA Novosti, 01.07.2026](https://ria.ru/20260701/turtsija-2102044608.html); [News.mail.ru](https://news.mail.ru/politics/71469017/). A forum user on an oncology-in-Turkey thread described a woman who collected money from Russians in Antalya: "На руках ни у нас, ни у других русских не было ни одной бумаги, ни договора с больницей, ни результатов исследований" (neither we nor the other Russians had a single document, a contract with the hospital or test results). — [Forum Vinsky (awd.ru), Turkey oncology thread](https://forum.awd.ru/viewtopic.php?t=148728&start=80)
- **Patients ask for itemised bills as a trust signal.** On the same forum, a patient praised a clinic that "дает развернутый счет на оплату, в котором подробно расписана стоимость каждой процедуры" (gives an itemised invoice with the cost of every procedure set out). — [awd.ru Turkey thread](https://forum.awd.ru/viewtopic.php?t=148728&start=40)

### Inferences
- The central trust failure is the gap between the quote and the final bill, combined with no accountable party once the patient is in India. A service that publishes binding, itemised quotes from several hospitals for the same diagnosis, states exclusions in plain Russian, and has a defined escalation path when the bill exceeds the quote would address the most-cited problem.
- Commission-based facilitation (20–35% in India, 30–70% in Korea) gives agents a reason to steer patients toward whoever pays most, not toward the best clinical fit. Disclosing commissions, or a flat fee paid by the patient, would set a new service apart.
- "Paid for a professor, saw a junior doctor" points to an unmet need: guaranteeing in writing which named consultant will see and operate on the patient.
- Because review sites are owned by facilitators and reviews are few, verified-patient reviews, ideally with a bill attached, are an open gap.

### Gaps
- Trustpilot, Google reviews of hospital international departments, otzovik, irecommend, Reddit and Telegram/VK groups could not be read. Searches returned no pages from these sites, and full-page fetching failed. No direct quotes from them are available.
- No quantitative data was found on how much final bills exceed estimates for foreign patients in India.
- There is no evidence on fake reviews specifically. Thinness and facilitator ownership of reviews can be shown, but not fabrication.

## 2. Travel and stay: visa, accommodation, food, interpreters, payments, transport, safety

### Takeaway
The best-documented failures are cheap, unsafe accommodation near hospitals and payment friction for Russians. In June 2026 a guesthouse fire next to Max Saket killed 21, including a Kyrgyz liver-transplant family. Russian payments run into sanctions-driven bank screening and non-working cards. That pushes patients to carry cash, which in turn invites theft: an Uzbek transplant patient was robbed of $16,000 in Gurugram. Informal translators, often from the patients' own region, act as the real support system when hospitals and agencies do not.

### Cited Findings
- **Fatal fire in medical-tourist housing (Delhi, 3 Jun 2026).** A fire at a B&B/guesthouse beside Max Super Speciality Hospital, Saket, killed 21 and injured 26. Authorities were investigating fire-safety and occupancy violations at the building (basement, ground and five floors). — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/21-killed-26-injured-in-delhi-hotel-fire-near-malviya-nagar-hospitals). Scroll reported that 12 of the dead were foreign nationals from South Asia and Africa, most of them patients or attendants. Hauz Rani is a "congested lal dora village (an urban enclave historically exempt from standard building bylaws)" next to the hospital. In Scroll's words, "the high cost of medical treatment and patchy insurance coverage compels patients and their caregivers to find cheap accommodation in this urban village." The AIIMS-run dharamshalas hold only a few hundred people, for one to three weeks. — [Scroll](https://amp.scroll.in/article/1093358/delhi-fire-deaths-highlight-precariousness-of-medical-tourists-in-the-city). Reports name the building as "Flourish Stay B&B" in Hauz Rani and as "Florist Inn" in Malviya Nagar. These appear to be the same event under different spellings; this is unconfirmed.
- **Three Kyrgyz citizens among the dead.** Nargiz Kochkarova of Osh had a liver transplant with her 23-year-old son as donor. Her mother came to care for them. Their treatment was complete, and they planned to fly home within a week (Ambassador Askar Beshimov). The embassy handled repatriation of the bodies. — [24.kg](https://24.kg/obschestvo/376495_semya_priehala_nalechenie_chto_izvestno_opogibshih_vdeli_kyirgyizstantsah/); [for.kg](https://www.for.kg/news-918922-ru.html); [Vesti.kg](https://vesti.kg/proisshestviya/item/150605-sredi-pogibshikh-vo-vremya-pozhara-v-gostinitse-v-nyu-deli-okazalis-chleny-odnoj-semi-iz-kyrgyzstana.html)
- **After the fire, translators did what hospitals and agencies did not.** When neighbouring guesthouses were sealed, translators, "not hospitals or medical-tourism companies," spent the night finding shelter for patients. — [ThePrint ground report](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/india-medical-tourism-medical-translators-delhi-hauz-rani-fire/2994214/) (via search excerpt); [Kashmir Observer, 5 Jun 2026](https://kashmirobserver.net/2026/06/05/translators-patient-guides-among-delhi-fire-victims/) reports that translators and patient guides were among the victims.
- **Informal translators run the parallel support system.** Translators, many of them refugees or migrants from the same countries as their patients (Afghanistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, DRC), book appointments, explain diagnoses and prescriptions, arrange accommodation and transport, handle follow-up appointments, and are the first point of contact in emergencies. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/india-medical-tourism-medical-translators-delhi-hauz-rani-fire/2994214/); [Medical Buyer](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/indias-medical-tourism-boom-over-5l-foreign-patients-arrived-last-year/). The same coverage cites 507,244 foreign nationals arriving for medical treatment in 2025 (more than 5 lakh).
- **Hospital interpreters turn into brokers.** An ethnography (Sept 2016–Mar 2019, four multi-specialty hospitals in Delhi and Gurgaon) followed Indian graduates in Russian who became hospital interpreters and then independent healthcare brokers. Delhi private hospitals have recruited Russian graduates since 2009, and also hire for Arabic, Farsi, French and Pashto. — [Muth & Suryanarayan, Multilingua 2020 (Lancaster eprint)](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/140721/2/Muth_Suryanarayan_2020.pdf) **[fieldwork OLDER]**. A companion pilot reportedly found Delhi private hospitals seeing 100–150 Russian-speaking patients a month. This figure comes from a summary page and was not verified. — [ResearchGate, "The Role of the Russian Language in India's Healthcare Sector"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319856963_THE_ROLE_OF_THE_RUSSIAN_LANGUAGE_IN_INDIA'S_HEALTHCARE_SECTOR)
- **Language is the most-cited barrier in the literature.** An integrative review found language barriers to be "the most common barrier, leading to miscommunication and anxiety." Clinicians were expected to speak more than one language because interpreters were in short supply. — [Integrative Review of Patients' Experience in Medical Tourism (PMC7285947)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7285947/). An Indian policy paper lists "lack of good language translators" among infrastructure challenges. — [IIPA paper](https://www.iipa.org.in/cms/public/uploads/522031635499677.pdf). In a single-hospital Indian study, non-Western patients had higher expectations of cultural and communication accommodation than Western patients, and the authors note that points may have been "lost in translation." — [ACHSM journal](https://journal.achsm.org.au/index.php/achsm/article/download/3801/1899/38133)
- **Visa friction.** Russian-language visa guides (mostly from agencies) say e-medical visas need a hospital invitation on letterhead with seals and signatures. Applications are refused when there is no English-language medical report from the home-country doctor. Attendants need proof of kinship and must be named in the hospital's invitation letter, and their visa is tied to the patient's. Extensions are possible only through FRRO and only for medical reasons. — [visy-v-indiyu.ru](https://www.visy-v-indiyu.ru/meditsinskaya-viza-v-indiyu); [blsindia-russia.ru](https://blsindia-russia.ru/medicinskaja-viza-v-indiju/); [Shifam Health 2026 guide](https://shifamhealth.com/meditsinskaya-viza-v-indiyu-iz-rossii-2026/) (one guide states that attendants need $50,000+ medical insurance; unverified). A guide for Tajik citizens lists refusal reasons: no referral from a local doctor, hospital letter not sent from an official email address, name mismatch, insufficient financial documents. — [Mycure Pathway (2026)](https://mycurepathway.com/medical-visa-for-india-from-tajikistan-russian/). Korea comparison: Kazakh travellers report uncertainty at immigration even with an approved K-ETA. — [Travie](https://en.travie.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=737)
- **Payments from Russia.** Large Indian banks apply strict compliance filters to Russian-origin payments because of the risk of US secondary sanctions. One guide estimates that 75–90% of SWIFT transfers are returned, with 2–6 weeks of manual checks (unverified). HDFC and ICICI apply enhanced compliance, while SBI is more stable via rupee Vostro accounts. Visa and Mastercard cards issued to Russians generally do not work in India, so guides advise virtual cards, several backup cards and cash rupees. — [vc.ru (2026)](https://vc.ru/opinions/2935954-kak-perevesti-dengi-v-indiyu-iz-rossii); [vc.ru cards guide](https://vc.ru/services/2860453-karty-v-indii-dlya-rossiyan-kak-platit-i-chto-rabotayut); [DTF guide](https://dtf.ru/craft/5031849-perevody-v-indiyu-iz-rossii)
- **Russian insurers and Indian clinics (Feb 2026), with conflicting accounts.** The tour-operator association ATOR said that from 2026 Indian commercial clinics, probably under sanctions pressure, stopped working directly with Russian assistance companies and that guarantees were blocked. The Russian Union of Travel Industry (RST) denied a ban and said settlements now go through authorised local partners paying in rupees, USD or cash. — [RBC, 21 Feb 2026](https://www.rbc.ru/society/21/02/2026/6999d9ed9a7947ab3aaf27ea); [RIA, 21 Feb 2026](https://ria.ru/20260221/indiya-2075990153.html)
- **Carrying cash leads to theft (Gurugram, Dec 2023).** An Uzbek national in Gurugram for a kidney transplant at Artemis Hospital was robbed of $16,000 by a gang posing as police in Sector 53. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/haryana/in-town-for-treatment-uzbek-national-robbed-of-16000-by-fake-cops-in-gurugram-2824796). The Uzbek embassy said it raised the case with India's MEA and other agencies. — [Dunyo.info](https://dunyo.info/ru/sootechestvenniki--/%D0%8Ezbekiston-elchihonasi-%D2%B2indistonning-gurugram-sha%D2%B3rida-vatandoshlarimiz-pulini-%D1%9Emarib-ketish-bilan-bo%D2%93li%D2%9B-vaziyatni-%D1%9Ez-nazoratiga-oldi)
- **Food.** The only evidence found is directory listings showing a cluster of Uzbek restaurants in Lajpat Nagar, Delhi, and a single Uzbek-tagged listing in Gurgaon. — [Justdial Lajpat Nagar](https://www.justdial.com/Delhi/Restaurants-for-Uzbek-in-Lajpat-Nagar/nct-10408936-att-res390)

### Inferences
- Vetted, fire-safe, affordable housing close to hospitals is a clear unmet need after June 2026. The cheapest housing next to Max Saket sits in a building-bylaw-exempt zone, and neither hospitals nor agencies took responsibility on the night of the fire. A service could publish a safety-audited housing list (fire exits, licences, occupancy limits) with Russian-speaking check-in.
- Payment rails are a real and current pain point for Russian citizens. Kazakh, Uzbek and Kyrgyz patients may face this less, but the evidence did not cover them. Legitimate rupee or local-partner settlement, plus escrow-style staged payments, would cut cash-carrying and robbery risk and give leverage in billing disputes.
- Interpreter quality and independence are both gaps. Hospital-employed interpreters work for the hospital, and many go on to become commissioned brokers. A credentialed, independent medical interpreter who answers to the patient is something no one in the evidence clearly offers.
- Visa requirements create document-preparation work (English medical reports, kinship proof for attendants, invitations from an official email). A service can take this on and reduce refusals.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on SIM cards, local transport scams, halal or Russian food availability in hospital canteens, loneliness of attendants, or safety for women travelling alone. A planned search on an assault reported involving an Uzbek woman in Delhi could not be run, so it is unknown whether she was a patient or attendant.
- The ThePrint and Scroll articles could not be read in full, so the translators' own quotes and their fee or commission arrangements are missing.
- No data on how widely Kazakh, Uzbek, Kyrgyz and Tajik bank cards are accepted in India.

## 3. In hospital: communication, consent, upselling, extended stays, ICU costs, deposits, billing disputes

### Takeaway
Evidence specific to foreign patients centres on deposits and prices rising once the patient is admitted, packages that exclude complications, ICU care or extra days, and poor communication. Indian law gives foreign patients the same remedies as citizens, and courts have said hospitals cannot hold patients over unpaid bills. Neither NABH nor any other body arbitrates disputes with facilitators.

### Cited Findings
- **Daily costs and upfront deposits exhaust families.** Tashkent case: $1,500–5,000/day demanded against roughly $1,000 expected; the family ran out of money and sought evacuation. — [EADaily 2019 [OLDER]](https://eadaily.com/ru/news/2019/06/12/bolshe-nichego-ne-bolit-grazhdanka-uzbekistana-umerla-na-lechenii-v-indii). Fortis transplant head: prices rise or upfront payment is demanded after arrival. — [Kun.uz 2023](https://kun.uz/news/2023/10/25/hind-shifokori-hindistonda-davolanishni-istagan-bemorlarga-tavsiyalar-berdi)
- **Delays while money runs out (Kazakhstan, May 2026).** A 16-year-old from Kyzylorda (Sunkar Sabit), diagnosed with testicular cancer in April, travelled to India through a Shymkent intermediary company. He died on his 12th day there without treatment having started. The reported cause of death was a vascular blockage, not the tumour. His mother, Raikhan Malikova, said the family gave the intermediaries 15–20 million tenge and that "за две недели организаторы лишь тратили деньги семьи, намеренно затягивая начало терапии" (for two weeks the organisers only spent the family's money, deliberately delaying the start of therapy). Headline quote: "С нас просто выкачивали деньги" (they were simply pumping money out of us). The company denies the accusations. — [Zakon.kz, 7 May 2026](https://www.zakon.kz/mir/6517102-s-nas-prosto-vykachivali-dengi-podrostok-iz-kyzylordy-umer-v-indii-ne-dozhdavshis-lecheniya.html); [Orda.kz](https://orda.kz/16-letnij-kazahstanec-umer-v-indii-ne-dozhdavshis-lechenija-ot-raka-414607/); [Newtimes.kz](https://newtimes.kz/obshchestvo/219004-podrostok-iz-kyzylordy-umer-v-indii-ot-onkologii-ne-dozhdavshis-lecheniia); [Azh.kz](https://azh.kz/ru/news/view/127605). (One search summary named the intermediary as "Medical Travel", but another said the name does not appear in the Zakon.kz text. Treat the company name as unconfirmed.)
- **Hospitals cannot hold patients over bills.** Indian legal commentary notes that the Delhi High Court held outstanding bills cannot be a reason to withhold a patient's release. — [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/can-hospitals-hold-patients-hostage-over-unpaid-bills). In a Kolkata case, the family alleged that the hospital demanded deposits before transfer; the hospital denied this and agreed to a refund. This was a domestic case. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/news/asia/india/patient-death-kolkata-hospital-denies-charges-to-refund-money-1.1983738)
- **Overbilling is a systemic issue in private hospitals.** A Chandigarh committee found a private hospital had overcharged a COVID patient by Rs 3.69 lakh and ordered a refund. This was a domestic case, but it shows that billing audits find real overcharges. — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/hospital-overcharges-rs-2-78l-in-chandigarh-272610)
- **Legal remedies are available on paper.** India's laws and case law offer foreign patients remedies "on an equal footing as the Indian citizens." — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=5db6db36-f992-45b8-aaae-977f1aacfb7f). But NABH, which empanels facilitators, "has no role to play" in disputes between patients and empanelled facilitators. Patients have filed negligence complaints or spoken out on social media instead. — [TD Appointments 2025 guide](https://tdappointments.com/blog/choosing-right-medical-tourism-facilitator-india-2025) (attribution taken from a search summary; it may instead be [IndHospitalSolution](https://indhospitalsolution.com/blogs/medical-tourism-consultancy-in-india-a-complete-guide))
- **Communication complaints.** One patient narrative described being unable to understand "the English of the average Indian." Complaints clustered around poor communication. — [JMIR 2020 [OLDER]](https://doi.org/10.2196/15665)
- **Praise for honest post-operative billing.** A Russian-language Medanta review notes that the doctor "дополнительные деньги не брал за приемы" (took no extra money for follow-up visits) after surgery. — [Oncoland Medanta](https://oncoland.ru/india/clinic/institut-medicinu-medanta)

### Inferences
- A patient-side "bill advocate" is a gap. That means daily review of the running bill in Russian, comparison against the quote, flags for unplanned procedures or ICU days, and a route to escalate before the family runs out of money. The Kyzylorda and Tashkent cases show that the time between admission and the start of treatment, and running daily costs, are where families lose money and trust.
- Clear rules for what happens when a package overruns (complications, ICU, extra nights), agreed before travel, would answer the "package" confusion described by the Fortis doctor.
- Because no body arbitrates facilitator disputes, a new service could offer its own contractual guarantee, such as a refund policy or a cap on the difference between quote and bill, as a trust differentiator.

### Gaps
- No documented foreign-patient cases of unconsented procedures or upselling in Indian hospitals were found.
- No data on ICU cost overruns for foreign patients specifically.
- The full texts of the Kyzylorda case could not be read, so the hospital name and the detailed timeline are missing.

## 4. After returning home: follow-up, discharge summaries, complications, medicines, teleconsultation, legal recourse, insurance

### Takeaway
After return is the least documented stage, which suggests it is also the least served. Agencies advertise remote follow-up and medicine delivery, but no independent evidence shows these work. Concerns about Indian drug quality in Uzbekistan, and Russian insurers' disrupted links with Indian clinics, add friction.

### Cited Findings
- **Follow-up is sold as an add-on, with no independent verification.** Kazakh and Russian agencies advertise that "после возвращения домой пациент продолжает дистанционное наблюдение… помогаем с доставкой препаратов и повторными онлайн-консультациями" (after returning home the patient continues remote monitoring… we help with delivering medicines and repeat online consultations), and delivery of "оригинальных индийских лекарств" (original Indian medicines) on prescription. — [IndiaMed.kz](https://indiamed.kz/); [Medturist.kz](https://medturist.kz/); [Deep-Med.ru](https://deep-med.ru/lechenie/indiya/)
- **Advice to arrange the handover before surgery.** A Russian guide advises agreeing a follow-up handover plan with the Indian clinic in advance: who will supply discharge summaries, prescriptions and rehabilitation instructions. — [FindHospital.com](https://findhospital.com/articles/osobennosti-lecheniya-v-indii/)
- **Bringing medicines home has its own rules.** Yandex Travel publishes a guide on which medicines can legally be brought back from India. — [Yandex Travel journal](https://travel.yandex.ru/journal/kakie-lekarstva-mozhno-privozit-iz-indii/)
- **Trust in Indian medicines is damaged in Uzbekistan.** Contaminated Indian cough syrup (Marion Biotech) was linked to the deaths of 65 children. An Uzbek court sentenced 23 people, including an Indian national, and prosecutors alleged bribes to skip inspections. — [Business Today, Aug 2023](https://www.businesstoday.in/industry/pharma/story/uzbekistan-says-contaminated-indian-cough-syrup-that-killed-65-children-was-on-market-due-to-bribery-report-394428-2023-08-17). Kun.uz also reported quality problems found in 50+ medicines in India in Sept 2024. — [Kun.uz, 27 Sep 2024](https://kun.uz/news/2024/09/27/hindistonda-50-dan-ortiq-dori-darmonlar-sifatida-muammo-aniqlandi)
- **Insurance.** Russian assistance and insurance guarantees with Indian clinics were reported blocked in 2026; RST disputed this (see Section 2). — [RBC](https://www.rbc.ru/society/21/02/2026/6999d9ed9a7947ab3aaf27ea); [RIA](https://ria.ru/20260221/indiya-2075990153.html)

### Inferences
- Bridging care back home is a large, under-served gap. That covers translating discharge summaries into Russian or local languages, mapping Indian brand-name drugs to their INN (generic names) and locally registered equivalents in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan, booking a local doctor in advance, and scheduling teleconsultations with the operating surgeon. Given the cough-syrup legacy, Uzbek patients in particular may refuse or distrust Indian-branded drugs, so mapping to local equivalents matters more there.
- Complication handling after return (who pays, who treats) does not appear in any agency promise that was found. A defined complication pathway would set a service apart.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on local doctors in Central Asia refusing or misunderstanding Indian discharge summaries, on complication rates after return, or on any legal action by Central Asian patients against Indian hospitals or facilitators.
- No evidence on which Indian brand-name drugs are unavailable in Kazakhstan or Uzbekistan.
- No evidence on whether teleconsultation follow-up actually happens. Searches returned only agency marketing.

## 5. Relatives' perspective: staying informed, decision making, money transfers, emotional support

### Takeaway
Relatives carry the financial risk (loans, crowdfunding) and often travel as donors or carers, which multiplies their exposure. In the documented cases they lacked an independent source of information and depended on the intermediary they later accused.

### Cited Findings
- **Families fund treatment through loans and crowdfunding.** Kazakh families borrow and crowdfund for cancer treatment abroad; one had spent 20 million tenge in India. — [Bes.media](https://bes.media/news/besplatno-no-pozdno-pochemu-kazahstantsi-sobirayut-dengi-na-lechenie-raka/)
- **Several family members travel, so a single disaster hits all of them.** The Kyrgyz liver-transplant case involved the patient, her donor son and her mother as carer; all three died in the Delhi fire. — [24.kg](https://24.kg/obschestvo/376495_semya_priehala_nalechenie_chto_izvestno_opogibshih_vdeli_kyirgyizstantsah/)
- **The mother in the Kyzylorda case saw the intermediary as draining money and delaying therapy, with no independent check.** — [Zakon.kz](https://www.zakon.kz/mir/6517102-s-nas-prosto-vykachivali-dengi-podrostok-iz-kyzylordy-umer-v-indii-ne-dozhdavshis-lecheniya.html)
- **Kyrgyz deaths abroad.** A Kyrgyz official said more and more citizens travel to Turkey and India for treatment, and that about 20–25 Kyrgyz citizens die in those countries each year. 174 bodies of citizens who died abroad were repatriated in the first five months of 2026. — [Vb.kg](https://www.vb.kg/doc/459209_s_nachala_goda_v_kyrgyzstan_dostavili_tela_174_grajdan_ymershih_za_rybejom.html)
- **Translators act as emotional support and first responders for families.** — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/india-medical-tourism-medical-translators-delhi-hauz-rani-fire/2994214/)

### Inferences
- An independent family dashboard is an open need: daily clinical status in Russian, the running bill against the quote, scheduled calls with the doctor, and an emergency contact other than the agent. None of the sources describes this being offered.
- Repatriation of remains, and emergency evacuation when money runs out, fall to embassies today. A defined repatriation and evacuation plan, possibly insured, is an unmet need.

### Gaps
- No direct relative testimony was found on remote decision making or the emotional burden. Telegram, VK and YouTube patient stories could not be searched.

## 6. Documented cases: bad experiences, scandals and praise

### Takeaway
Recent (2023–2026) documented Central Asian cases involve intermediary-linked financial abuse and delays (Kyzylorda, 2026), unsafe housing deaths (Kyrgyz family, 2026) and robbery of cash-carrying patients (Uzbek, 2023). Organ-transplant rackets involving foreign recipients (mostly Bangladeshi so far) have led to stricter, slower approvals for foreign live-donor transplants. Praise centres on modern facilities, competent doctors, and good interpreters when the patient has one.

### Cited Findings
- **Kazakhstan, May 2026.** 16-year-old from Kyzylorda died in India before treatment began; the family accuses a Shymkent intermediary. — [Zakon.kz](https://www.zakon.kz/mir/6517102-s-nas-prosto-vykachivali-dengi-podrostok-iz-kyzylordy-umer-v-indii-ne-dozhdavshis-lecheniya.html)
- **Kyrgyzstan, June 2026.** Three members of one family (liver recipient, son as donor, mother as carer) killed in the Delhi guesthouse fire next to Max Saket. — [24.kg](https://24.kg/obschestvo/376495_semya_priehala_nalechenie_chto_izvestno_opogibshih_vdeli_kyirgyizstantsah/); [Kaktus.media](https://kaktus.media/doc/547404_pri_pojare_v_indii_pogibli_troe_kyrgyzstancev.html)
- **Uzbekistan, Dec 2023.** Kidney-transplant patient robbed of $16,000 by fake police in Gurugram. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/haryana/in-town-for-treatment-uzbek-national-robbed-of-16000-by-fake-cops-in-gurugram-2824796)
- **Uzbekistan, 2019 [OLDER].** Tashkent cancer patient died; son accused intermediaries of misquoting costs. — [EADaily](https://eadaily.com/ru/news/2019/06/12/bolshe-nichego-ne-bolit-grazhdanka-uzbekistana-umerla-na-lechenii-v-indii)
- **Transplant rackets involving foreign patients.**
  - Delhi police (July 2024) arrested seven people, including a transplant surgeon at Indraprastha Apollo who allegedly performed 15–16 transplants at Yatharth Hospital, Noida, for Bangladeshi patients. Donors were reportedly paid Rs 4–5 lakh while recipients were charged Rs 25–30 lakh, and documents in the name of the Bangladesh High Commission were allegedly forged to show a donor–recipient relationship. — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/doctors/bangladesh-india-kidney-transplant-racket-indraprastha-apollo-surgeon-held-for-running-racket-at-yatharth-hospital-131358); [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/asia/india/news/kidney-transplant-racket-involved-bangladeshi-patients-delhi-police-3653441); [AIR News](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/delhi-seven-arrested-in-connection-with-international-organ-transplant-racket)
  - In Kanpur (2026), five doctors were among six people arrested. Police said the network may have done 40–50 illegal transplants, including on foreigners, with links to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Nepal. — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/doctors/illegal-kidney-transplant-racket-in-kanpur-five-doctors-among-6-arrested-167728)
  - In Karnataka, after a racket was busted in Ramanagar, foreign patients wait far longer for live-kidney transplant approval. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/foreigners-wait-longer-kidney-transplant-2144608)
  - Indian law (THOTA 1994, amended 2011 and 2014) limits live donation to close relatives. Foreign patients need embassy or government certification of the donor relationship, sometimes with DNA tests, and missing paperwork can mean refusal even when donor and patient are medically compatible. — [Qonaq Health (commercial)](https://www.qonaqhealth.com/ru/blog/who-can-get-an-organ-transplant-in-india-eligibility-rules-explained); [MedicaTour](https://medicatour.online/stati/transplantacziya-organov-v-indii-ot-konvejera-nadezhdyi-do-eticheskix-labirintov)
- **Turkey, July 2026.** Prepayment scams aimed at Russians (fake check-up offers). — [RIA](https://ria.ru/20260701/turtsija-2102044608.html)
- **What patients praise.**
  - Artemis: "чисто, ухожено, современное оборудование, вежливый медперсонал" (clean, well kept, modern equipment, polite medical staff), with better value for money than at home. — [Bookimed Artemis](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/clinic/artemis-health-institute/)
  - A Kazakh family reported almost no problems with the quality of Indian doctors. — [Bes.media](https://bes.media/news/besplatno-no-pozdno-pochemu-kazahstantsi-sobirayut-dengi-na-lechenie-raka/)
  - A reviewer wrote that "языковой барьер – его решил переводчик" (the interpreter solved the language barrier). — [Oncoland Medanta page](https://oncoland.ru/india/clinic/institut-medicinu-medanta)
  - A forum user cited accreditation, US-trained doctors and costs "several times lower." — [Forum Vinsky (awd.ru)](https://forum.awd.ru/viewtopic.php?t=319890) [OLDER, about 2016]
  - Korea: patients from Kazakhstan praise airport pickup, housing near the clinic and interpreters ("без их помощи было бы невозможно общаться с профессором и медперсоналом", without their help it would have been impossible to talk to the professor and medical staff). These are agency-hosted reviews. — [Korea-medservis](https://korea-medservis.ru/index.php/otzyvy); [MJ Russko](http://mjrussko.com/reports)

### Inferences
- Transplant patients, a core Central Asian segment (kidney and liver with family donors), face the heaviest paperwork and scrutiny. Racket crackdowns have lengthened approval times. Preparing embassy certificates, kinship and DNA proof and committee files correctly is a concrete, valuable service, and it also protects patients from being drawn into illegal arrangements.
- The praise is mainly for clinical quality and facilities. The complaints are mainly about intermediaries, money and logistics. This supports the idea that the gap lies in the non-clinical layer around the hospital, not in the hospitals' clinical capability.

### Gaps
- No Central Asian or Russian patient was found named in an Indian organ-trafficking case in this session.
- The Apollo/Myanmar "cash-for-kidneys" allegations (UK press, Dec 2023) were not checked in this session because the search budget ran out. They are known from background knowledge only and should not be cited without checking.
- No documented deaths or complications of Central Asian patients in Turkey or Korea were checked, apart from the Kyrgyz statistic that 20–25 die yearly in Turkey and India combined.

## 7. Which problems are already partially solved, and by whom

### Takeaway
Pieces exist: hospital interpreters and international desks, voluntary NABH facilitator empanelment, a planned Heal in India portal with ratings, agency packages covering visa, interpreter and housing, and Korea's registered-agent regime. But no single actor is independent of commissions and accountable end to end, from quote to post-return care. Nothing found covers safe housing standards, bill advocacy, family dashboards or the handover back home.

### Cited Findings
- **Hospitals.** Many Indian hospitals offer interpreters for Arabic, French, Russian and other languages, plus liaison officers. — [KareTrip](https://karetrip.com/blogs/guidelines-for-international-patients-in-indian-hospitals); [Artemis medical interpreter services](https://www.artemishospitals.com/about-us/services/medical-interpreter-services). Apollo runs a representative office in Uzbekistan. — [apollo.uz](http://apollo.uz/). (Limitation: these interpreters are paid by the hospital, and some become brokers; see Muth & Suryanarayan above.)
- **Indian government.** The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare / PIB (May 2026) says it is "encouraging the registration and rating of Medical Value Travel (MVT) facilitators to build international trust." It plans a revamped MVT portal and a National Medical & Wellness Tourism Promotion Board. — [PIB, 2 May 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/may/doc202652862301.pdf). NABH runs a voluntary MVTF empanelment programme, with 2nd-edition standards released in 2025. — [NABH MVTF](https://nabh.co/programmes/medical-value-travel-facilitator-mvtf-empanelment-programme/); [NABH MVTF Standards 2nd ed.](https://nabh-portal-live.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/24070800/MVTF-Standards-2nd-Edition.pdf). KPMG (July 2025): the Heal in India portal would include service ratings, and registered facilitators would get visibility. — [KPMG Heal in India](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/07/heal-in-india-catalysing-medical-and-wellness-tourism-for-a-healthier-global-future.pdf). No mandatory licensing of facilitators was found, and NABH does not arbitrate disputes (see Section 3).
- **Facilitators and aggregators.** Vaidam says it charges patients no commission, works as a partner of hospitals, and has an office in Uzbekistan. This is a self-claim. — [Vaidam Uzbekistan](https://www.vaidam.com/ru/uzbekistan). Kazakh agencies advertise a coordinator and interpreter, document translation, remote follow-up and medicine delivery. — [IndiaMed.kz FAQ](https://indiamed.kz/vopros-otvet); [Medturist.kz](https://medturist.kz/). Aggregators (Bookimed, ClinicsOnCall, Hospitals-travel.ru, MediGlobus) show prices and reviews, but review volumes are small (Section 1).
- **Translators and informal fixers.** They fill gaps in housing, transport and emergencies, but without formal standing. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/india-medical-tourism-medical-translators-delhi-hauz-rani-fire/2994214/)
- **Payments.** Authorised local partners settle in rupees, USD or cash (RST). — [RIA](https://ria.ru/20260221/indiya-2075990153.html). SBI rupee Vostro routes. — [DTF](https://dtf.ru/craft/5031849-perevody-v-indiyu-iz-rossii)
- **Korea, as a model.** Mandatory registration of patient-referral agents (Medical Act Art. 27-3) and a government complaint channel (1372) for foreign patients. — [Korea Clinic Guide](https://www.koreaclinicguide.com/blog/patient-rights-raise-dispute-international-patients); [Diaad](https://www.diaad.co.kr/en/insights/how-korea-s-registered-patient-agent-rules-split-the-medical)
- **Competitor destination.** Uzbekistan is trying to become a regional medical-tourism hub, including dedicated border "corridors" for patients, while officials admit "медицина находится не на том уровне" (medicine is not at the right level) and many Uzbeks still go abroad for complex surgery. — [Azattyq Asia, Nov 2024](https://www.azattyqasia.org/a/33198345.html); [Knews.kg](https://knews.kg/2024/11/13/uzbekistan-stremitsya-stat-tsentrom-meditsinskogo-turizma-v-tsentralnoj-azii/)

### Inferences
Pain points that existing players do not appear to solve, ranked by strength of evidence:
1. **Quote-to-bill integrity**, meaning binding itemised quotes, clear package exclusions and an escalation path. Strong evidence: Kun.uz 2023, Kyzylorda 2026, EADaily 2019. Partly addressed by aggregators' price lists, but not binding.
2. **Agent accountability and commission conflicts**: disclosure, escrow or staged payments, refund guarantees. Strong evidence: intermediary disappearance, Turkey prepayment scams, 20–35% and 30–70% commission figures. NABH empanelment is voluntary and does not resolve disputes.
3. **Safe, vetted accommodation near hospitals.** Strong evidence: June 2026 fire. Not addressed by hospitals or agencies.
4. **Payment rails without cash, especially for Russians.** Strong evidence: sanctions screening, card failures, the $16k robbery. Partly addressed by local-partner settlement.
5. **Independent, credentialed interpreters who answer to the patient.** Moderate evidence. Hospitals provide interpreters but they are conflicted. Informal translators fill gaps but are unregulated.
6. **Transplant documentation support** (embassy certificates, kinship and DNA proof, committee files). Moderate evidence. Stricter scrutiny after the rackets raises the stakes.
7. **Handover back home**: translated discharge summaries, INN drug mapping, local doctor booking, teleconsultation, a complication pathway. Weak direct evidence but a clear absence. Agencies advertise it but nothing verifies it.
8. **Family transparency dashboard and evacuation or repatriation plan.** Weak direct evidence. Repatriation is currently left to embassies.

### Gaps
- No search covered the independent review sites (Trustpilot, Google Maps reviews of the international desks at Medanta, Artemis, Fortis, Max and Apollo), Reddit r/medicaltourism, YouTube patient vlogs, or Russian-language Telegram/VK groups ("лечение в Индии" chats). This was because full-page fetching failed and the shared search budget was exhausted. A follow-up pass on those sources would add first-person quotes on interpreter quality, food, loneliness, women's safety and follow-up care, which are currently thin.
- Turkmenistan and Tajikistan-specific cases were not found (no Asia-Plus coverage surfaced).
- IMTJ coverage of India patient experience was not retrieved.
